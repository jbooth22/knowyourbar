The 10 Best Vegan Protein Bars

A vegan protein bar has no animal ingredients at all: no whey, milk, honey, eggs, collagen, casein or gelatin. Most mainstream bars are built on whey or milk protein, so going vegan usually means pea, rice or soy protein instead. We use the vegan label on every bar, then read the ingredient list ourselves.

Of the 1,000+ bars we track, 281 are vegan, about 21%. They grade A or B more often than the average bar (54% vs. 46%), but carry less protein on average.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

Pea protein is the plant-protein default.
128 of the 281 vegan bars use pea protein, ahead of brown rice protein (102) and soy protein (49).
Vegan bars grade better but carry less protein.
54% of vegan bars grade A or B, against 46% database-wide. They average 10.2g of protein against 13.3g, since whey and milk protein are easier to pack into a bar than plant protein.
Vegan and clean are different questions.
Barebells' four vegan flavors grade D to F, worse than its dairy-based lineup. A vegan label doesn't guarantee a clean ingredient list.

How we picked these

What qualifies: The bar is marked vegan (the Vegan field in our database). We also read every ingredient list for whey, milk protein, honey, collagen, casein, egg whites and gelatin, to explain why the rest miss. 281 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Highest fiber: The most fiber, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best gluten-free: Labeled gluten free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best soy-free: Labeled soy free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest net carbs: The fewest net carbs (total carbs minus fiber minus sugar alcohol), grade B or better, 10g+ protein.

How the A to F grades themselves are calculated: our scoring methodology. Who built this and why: About Know Your Bar.
