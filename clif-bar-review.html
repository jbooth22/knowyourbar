Are Clif Bars Healthy? We Ranked All 28 Flavors Across 3 Product Lines

Clif isn't one bar, it's three separate product lines wearing the same name. Clif Builders is the actual protein bar and ranges from grade A to F depending on flavor. CLIF Bar is a lower-protein energy bar that never breaks C, and Clif Kid ZBar is a children's snack bar that never breaks D.

Rather than labeling macros good or bad, we rank Clif against every bar in our database so you can see where they stand in the full market. These figures are averaged across all 28 flavors from all three Clif lines; see the line-by-line breakdown below for how CLIF Bar, Clif Builders, and Clif ZBar differ from each other.

Averaged across all three lines, Clif runs 12.0g of protein and 14.5g of sugar per bar, numbers pulled down by CLIF Bar and Clif ZBar. Judged on Clif Builders alone, the picture is very different: 17.2g protein average with two flavors scoring in the top ingredient-quality tier.

What the data shows across all 28 Clif flavors

Clif sells three distinctly different bars under one brand name, and they don't perform anywhere near the same. Clif Builders averages 17.2g of protein per bar, close to 17g more per serving than CLIF Bar's average of 10.2g and more than three times Clif ZBar's 5.0g. Only Builders is really built as a protein bar. CLIF Bar is closer to a classic energy bar, and Clif ZBar is explicitly marketed as Clif Kid ZBar, a children's snack, not an adult protein product.

Ingredient quality tracks the same split. Clif Builders holds the only two A grades in the entire family, Peanut Butter Chocolate and Crispy Almond Salted Caramel, and both lead their ingredient lists with soy protein isolate and whey protein concentrate ahead of any sweetener. The rest of the Builders line falls off a cliff: its seven F-grade flavors front-load three separate sugar sources, cane syrup, brown rice syrup, and cane sugar, before the protein even shows up. CLIF Bar never reaches this low, but it never reaches higher than a C either, topping out with Trail Mix at a modest 1.5 score. None of the 28 flavors across any line use artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols.

Ingredient quality patterns across the Clif lineup

These patterns hold across all 28 flavors from all three lines combined. Every single Clif flavor gets credit for a quality protein source, but that's true broadly across the category. What actually separates a strong flavor from a weak one is whether "Protein Leads," meaning protein shows up before sweeteners in the ingredient list, which only happens in about a third of Clif flavors, concentrated almost entirely in Builders.

Bottom line

Clif isn't one product to judge, it's three. Clif Builders is the only line worth buying for a genuine protein bar, and even then only about half its flavors are worth reaching for: the two A-grade flavors lead with real protein sources, while its seven F-grade flavors stack three sugar sources ahead of the protein. CLIF Bar is a middling energy bar, never bad enough to avoid outright but never good enough to recommend over a dedicated protein bar. Clif ZBar isn't really part of this conversation at all, it's a kids' snack bar sold under the Clif name, and by ingredient quality it's the weakest of the three lines.

If you're shopping the Clif lineup specifically for a protein bar, go straight to Clif Builders and check the flavor before you buy, since the gap between its best and worst flavor is the widest of any brand we've reviewed. Don't assume "Clif" on the label means the bar you're holding is anything like the others.
