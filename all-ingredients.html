Frequently asked questions

This encyclopedia covers 592 ingredients that each appear in 5 or more of the 1,308 protein bars in our database. Our full scoring schema has 1,300+ canonical ingredients, but we cut off at 5 bars to keep the reference useful rather than exhaustive.
The share of all 1,308 bars in our database that contain that ingredient anywhere on the label, including inside a compound ingredient like a chocolate coating. Salt, for example, appears in 83% of bars. Maltitol appears in 13%. The percentage is out of every bar in the database, not just the category.
Scores run from -4 (worst) to +4 (best). A score of 0 is neutral. Positive scores indicate beneficial or clean ingredients. Negative scores indicate ingredients that drag down a bar's overall grade. The scores reflect ingredient quality in the context of a protein bar, not an absolute health judgment.
Natural flavors is a broad regulatory category that can include hundreds of substances. The term reveals nothing about what specific compounds are present. We score it as -1 because it adds no nutritional value and provides no transparency about what you are actually consuming. It is not a major concern, but it is not a positive signal either.
Maltitol (-4) has a glycemic index around 35, significantly higher than erythritol (-3), which is close to zero. Maltitol also causes more GI distress than most sugar alcohols. Erythritol is largely absorbed before it reaches the colon and has a far lower impact on blood sugar. Both are sugar alcohols, but they behave very differently in the body.
They are starches that were rearranged so the body can't fully digest them. That lets a label count them as fiber and lower "net carbs." They aren't harmful, but they are highly processed stand-ins for fiber from food, so they score -1. Fiber pulled from plants, like chicory root fiber and inulin, scores +1.
Glycerin is a humectant used to keep bars soft and extend shelf life. It has a mild sweetness and a small caloric impact. We score it at -1 because it is a processing additive with no nutritional benefit, but it is not a red flag ingredient. It appears in nearly half of all bars (49%), which shows how central it is to the texture and shelf life of the modern protein bar.
Palm kernel oil is the fat of choice for chocolate-style coatings because it creates a firm snap at room temperature without refrigeration. It appears in 283 bars, and in 106 of them it only shows up inside a compound ingredient like "chocolate flavored coating" rather than on its own, which is why it's easy to miss. It scores -3 because it is a highly saturated, industrially processed fat with environmental concerns around sourcing.
Scores reflect completeness of amino acid profile and processing level. Whey protein isolate and egg whites score +4 because they are complete proteins with excellent bioavailability and minimal processing. Collagen scores +1 because while it is technically protein, it lacks tryptophan and is not a complete protein source. Pea and brown rice protein score +2 as clean plant sources that, when combined, provide a complete amino acid profile.
