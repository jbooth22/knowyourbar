Frequently asked questions

This encyclopedia covers 369 ingredients that each appear in 5 or more bars across our database of 1,000+ protein bars. There are over 1,000 unique canonical ingredients in our full schema, but we cut off at 5 appearances to keep the reference meaningful rather than exhaustive.
The bar represents what percentage of the 1,000+ bars in our database contain that ingredient. Salt, for example, appears in about 46% of bars. Maltitol appears in about 11%. The percentage is out of 1,000 total bars, not just the category total.
Scores run from -4 (worst) to +4 (best). A score of 0 is neutral. Positive scores indicate beneficial or clean ingredients. Negative scores indicate ingredients that drag down a bar's overall grade. The scores reflect ingredient quality in the context of a protein bar, not an absolute health judgment.
Natural flavors is a broad regulatory category that can include hundreds of substances. The term reveals nothing about what specific compounds are present. We score it as -1 because it adds no nutritional value and provides no transparency about what you are actually consuming. It is not a major concern, but it is not a positive signal either.
Maltitol has a glycemic index around 35 — significantly higher than erythritol, which is close to zero. Maltitol also causes more GI distress than most sugar alcohols. Erythritol is largely absorbed before it reaches the colon and has a far lower impact on blood sugar. Both are sugar alcohols, but they behave very differently in the body.
Glycerin is a humectant used to keep bars soft and extend shelf life. It has a mild sweetness and a small caloric impact. We score it at -1 because it is a processing additive with no nutritional benefit, but it is not a red flag ingredient. Its appearance in nearly 1 in 3 bars reflects how central it is to the texture and shelf stability of the modern protein bar.
Palm kernel oil is the fat of choice for chocolate-style coatings because it creates a firm snap at room temperature without refrigeration. It appears in 143 bars — almost always inside a compound ingredient like "dark chocolate coating" or "chocolate flavored coating" rather than listed directly. That is why consumers rarely notice it. It scores -3 because it is a highly saturated, industrially processed fat with environmental concerns around sourcing.
Scores reflect completeness of amino acid profile and processing level. Whey protein isolate and egg whites score +4 because they are complete proteins with excellent bioavailability and minimal processing. Collagen protein scores +1 because while it is technically protein, it lacks tryptophan and is not a complete protein source. Pea and brown rice protein score +2 as clean plant sources that, when combined, provide a complete amino acid profile.
