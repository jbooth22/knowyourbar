What disqualifies a protein bar from being kosher

We check the Kosher (Y/N) label on file for every bar. Kosher is different from Vegan, Gluten Free, Dairy Free, and Soy Free: it's a supervised-process certification, not primarily an ingredient screen. Whey, milk, soy, wheat, sugar, and nuts can all be kosher when produced and supervised correctly, so an ingredient's mere presence usually doesn't disqualify a bar the way whey disqualifies Dairy Free. 149 of 1,307 bars, about 11%, carry a kosher label. The other 1158, about 89%, don't.

We do cross-check ingredient lists for three ingredients that are almost never kosher without their own specific certification: gelatin (77 bars, 5.9%), confectioner's glaze or shellac (14 bars, 1.1%), and carmine or rennet (5 bars, 0.4%). Together those account for only a small share of the 1158 bars that aren't labeled kosher. The remaining 1070 bars, 81.9% of the full database, show none of those ingredients at all. They simply haven't been through kosher certification, which is a different claim from containing something non-kosher.
