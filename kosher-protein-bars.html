The 10 Best Kosher Protein Bars

A kosher protein bar is certified by a supervising agency, usually with a symbol like OU or Star-K on the wrapper. It's a claim about how the bar is made and supervised, not just what's in it. We use the kosher label on every bar, then flag the few ingredients that are almost never kosher on their own.

Of the 1,000+ bars we track, 154 are certified kosher, about 12%. Most of the rest aren't certified rather than made with something non-kosher: only 8% contain gelatin, confectioner's glaze, carmine, or rennet.

What keeps a protein bar from being kosher

Kosher is different from vegan, gluten free, dairy free or soy free. It's a supervised-process certification, not an ingredient screen: whey, milk, soy, wheat, sugar and nuts can all be kosher when they're produced and supervised correctly. So we use the kosher label on file for every bar. 154 of 1,000+ bars, about 12%, carry it. The other 1,154 don't.

We do check ingredient lists for three ingredients that are almost never kosher without their own certification. Together they show up in only 88 of the 1,154 bars without the label. The other 1,066 (92%) name none of them: the brand just hasn't sought certification.

Gelatin
77 bars6%
Usually from pork or from animals not slaughtered under kosher rules, used in the puff or crisp layer of several bars. Fish and certified-kosher beef gelatin exist, but plain "gelatin" on a US label usually isn't kosher.
Found in: 1st Phorm, Anabar, Atkins, BalanceDiet, Battle Bars, Built, David, Equate, FITCRUNCH, Fulfil, GNC Total Lean, Gryp, Melo, MTN OPS, Musashi, Pure Protein
Confectioner's glaze / shellac
14 bars1%
A resin made by the lac insect, used as a shiny coating on candy pieces and drizzles. Not kosher without its own certification.
Found in: Anabar, Built, FITCRUNCH, MyProtein, One, Pure Protein
Carmine or rennet
5 bars<1%
Carmine (an insect-derived red color) and rennet (traditionally animal-derived, used in some dairy blends). The rarest of the three.
Found in: David, Legendary, Musashi
Not kosher-certified
1,066 bars81%
No gelatin, confectioner's glaze, carmine, or rennet anywhere in the ingredient list we can find. The brand simply hasn't pursued kosher certification, which is a claim about how a bar is made and supervised, not just what's in it.

Kosher doesn't screen on ingredient grade

Kosher says nothing about ingredient quality, so 85 of the 154 kosher bars here grade C or below. Every Best 10 pick is an A or B, and our Top 50 lists A and B bars first.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

Not being kosher is almost always about certification, not ingredients.
Only 8% of bars without the kosher label contain gelatin, confectioner's glaze, carmine, or rennet. The other 92% just haven't sought certification.
Gelatin is the clearest non-kosher ingredient.
77 bars list plain gelatin, more than the other flagged ingredients combined. Built is the example: all 13 flavors use it and none are kosher.
Kosher bars grade about average but carry less protein.
45% of kosher bars grade A or B, against 48% database-wide. They average 7.7g of protein against 13.3g, because the kosher-certified set leans toward snack-style bars like Larabar and Bobo's.

How we picked these

What qualifies: The bar carries a kosher label (the Kosher field in our database). Kosher is a supervised-process certification, so we can't verify it from the ingredient list; we also flag gelatin, confectioner's glaze and carmine or rennet to explain why some bars miss. Ingredient grade is not part of the kosher screen. 154 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify, 69 of them with an A or B grade.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best gluten-free: Labeled gluten free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best non-GMO: Labeled Non-GMO by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
