How we define it

Our criteria: ≥15g protein and ≤5g sugar

The protein bar market uses "high protein" loosely. We drew a firm line: 15g of protein minimum and 5g of sugar maximum. These numbers reflect a meaningful threshold, the average bar in our database has 18g protein and 9g sugar, so bars hitting both criteria are genuinely above average on both dimensions.

240 of 900+ bars meet the criteria. Of those, 25 earn an A grade from our ingredient quality scoring system, meaning they achieve the macro profile without relying on low-quality ingredients to get there.

High Protein: 15g or more
The average protein bar has 18g of protein. Our threshold of 15g represents the lower bound of what most people would consider a "high protein" bar. Bars above 20g are labeled specifically in our tool.

Low Sugar: 5g or less
The average bar has 9g of sugar. Five grams or less means this bar is using something other than sugar as its primary sweetener usually dates, sugar alcohols, or artificial sweeteners. We flag which approach each bar uses.

The trade-off most bars make
To hit high protein with low sugar and few calories, most bars use artificial sweeteners (sucralose, acesulfame) or sugar alcohols (erythritol, maltitol). 129 of the 240 qualifying bars use at least one. We flag them clearly. Whether you care about that is up to you.

Clean options exist
111 bars hit the protein and sugar thresholds without any artificial sweeteners, and 73 do it without any sugar alcohols either. Gryp, Linear, Munk Pack, B.T.R. Nation, and Promix are standouts that achieve both.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What counts as low sugar high protein for a protein bar?
Our definition: at least 15g of protein and no more than 5g of sugar per bar. Of the 900+ bars we scored, 240 meet both criteria. The average protein bar has 18g protein and 9g sugar, so these bars are meaningfully better on both dimensions. 'High protein' and 'low sugar' are relative if you need a stricter threshold, our tool lets you set exact slider values.
Can you get high protein without artificial sweeteners in low sugar bars?
Yes, but it takes more looking. Of the 240 low-sugar high-protein bars in our database, 111 (46%) contain no artificial sweeteners. Options include Linear (20g protein, 3g sugar, no artificial sweeteners), Gryp (25g protein, 2g sugar, no artificial sweeteners), and Munk Pack (15g protein, 1g sugar, no artificial sweeteners). The most efficient high-protein low-sugar bars Quest and Barebells. all use artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols to achieve their sweetness profile.
Why do low sugar protein bars usually contain artificial sweeteners?
Because sweetness and protein efficiency pull in opposite directions. Sugar provides sweetness at 4 calories per gram. To hit 20g of protein at 200 calories or less, there is almost no caloric budget left for sugar. Manufacturers fill the sweetness gap with zero-calorie sweeteners. The bars on this list that avoid this trade-off tend to use fewer sweet flavors or accept a slightly higher calorie count.
What is the highest protein bar with no sugar?
Gryp Peanut Butter Choco Chunk has 25g of protein and 2g of sugar with no artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols, the best combination of all three in our database. Munk Pack Cookie Dough has 15g protein and only 1g sugar, also with no artificial sweeteners. For pure protein with near-zero sugar at any cost, David bars hit 28g protein at 150 calories but use acesulfame potassium.
Are low sugar protein bars good for weight loss?
Low sugar bars reduce one source of excess calories but the total calorie count matters more for weight management than sugar alone. A 200-calorie bar with 20g protein and 2g sugar is typically a better choice than a 300-calorie bar with 20g protein and 2g sugar for calorie-focused goals.
