What disqualifies a protein bar from being dairy free

We check the Dairy Free (Y/N) label on file for every bar, then cross-check ingredient lists ourselves for whey, milk, and casein, the three named dairy sources that show up most in the data. 247 of 1,308 bars, about 19%, carry a dairy free label. The other 1,061, about 81%, don't.

Whey is the most common named source at 533 bars (40.7% of the full database), usually as the bar's main protein. Milk shows up in 454 bars (34.7%), and casein in 201 bars (15.4%). The remaining 459 bars, 35.1% of the full database, show no whey, milk, or casein in their own ingredient list at all. They just aren't labeled dairy free, which is a different claim from actually containing dairy.

459 of the 1,308 bars we track have no whey, milk, or casein anywhere in their own ingredient list, but the brand hasn't labeled or certified them dairy free. Not being labeled dairy free is not the same as containing dairy.
Whey is the clearest single dairy source.
533 bars (40.7% of the database) name whey directly, more than milk or casein on its own.
Quest shows the pattern clearly.
16 of Quest's 16 flavors use whey directly. None of Quest's flavors are labeled dairy free.
39 brands are dairy free across their entire lineup.
Led by Aloha (18), Fro Pro (15), Trubar (15), and IQ Bar (10), these brands build around a plant, egg, or collagen protein source from the start, rather than swapping whey out flavor by flavor.
Dairy free bars grade noticeably better than the database average.
70.9% of dairy free bars grade A or B, against 54.9% database-wide.
Dairy free bars average less protein, more fiber.
Dairy free bars average 11.1g of protein against a database-wide average of 13.3g, and 6.5g of fiber vs. 5.0g.
Mixed lineups are the exception, not the rule.
Kize is the clearest large example: 9 of 12 flavors are dairy free, the rest are not. Always check the specific flavor, not just the brand.
