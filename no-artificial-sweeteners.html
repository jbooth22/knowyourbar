What counts as an artificial sweetener?

The term "artificial sweetener" gets used loosely. For this list, we use a strict definition: synthetic compounds added to food to provide sweetness with no caloric contribution. There are four that appear in protein bars. Every bar on this page contains none of them.

Sucralose
Also listed as: Splenda
The most common artificial sweetener in protein bars by far. Made by chlorinating sugar. Approximately 600x sweeter than sugar. Stable at high temperatures, making it popular in baked and coated bars.
Found in 181 of 898 bars (22%)
Acesulfame Potassium
Also listed as: Acesulfame-K, Ace-K
Often used alongside sucralose to enhance sweetness. About 200x sweeter than sugar. Rarely appears alone in protein bars — typically a supporting sweetener in bars already using sucralose.
Found in 12 of 898 bars (1.5%)
Aspartame
Also listed as: NutraSweet, Equal
Widely used in beverages and older diet products. Less heat-stable than sucralose, which limits its use in baked bars. Found in very few modern protein bars — aspartame has largely fallen out of favor with bar manufacturers.
Found in 0 of 898 bars
Saccharin
Also listed as: Sweet'N Low
The oldest artificial sweetener, rarely used in protein bars today. Has a distinct metallic aftertaste at high concentrations. Effectively absent from the modern protein bar market.
Found in 0 of 898 bars
What about sugar alcohols? Sugar alcohols (erythritol, maltitol, xylitol, sorbitol) are a separate category. They are carbohydrate-derived, not synthetic, and they do contribute some calories. They are not artificial sweeteners by definition, though some people prefer to avoid them too. This page focuses exclusively on artificial sweeteners. If you want bars free of both, see our clean protein bars guide — those bars avoid artificial sweeteners, sugar alcohols, and have an A or B ingredient grade.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What artificial sweeteners are in protein bars?
The four artificial sweeteners found in protein bars are sucralose, acesulfame potassium (acesulfame-K), aspartame, and saccharin. Sucralose is by far the most common — it appears in 181 of the 898 bars we scored. Acesulfame potassium appears in 12 bars, usually alongside sucralose. Aspartame and saccharin appear in zero bars in our current database. The modern protein bar market has shifted almost entirely to sucralose as its synthetic sweetener of choice.
What protein bars have no artificial sweeteners?
Brands with no artificial sweeteners across their entire lineup include RXBAR, Perfect Bar, Larabar, Bearded Bros, Jonesbar, Linear, Kize, GoMacro, and Daryl's Bars. Of the 898 bars we scored, 632 (78%) contain no artificial sweeteners at all. The top-ranked options by ingredient quality score include RXBAR (10 of 12 flavors earn an A grade), Perfect Bar, Linear, and Gryp.
Is sucralose in protein bars bad for you?
Sucralose is FDA-approved and has a long safety record at typical consumption levels. The research picture is mixed — some studies suggest potential effects on gut microbiome composition at high doses, while others show no meaningful impact. Our ingredient scoring system rates sucralose as a concern ingredient (not a dangerous one), which is why bars containing it tend to score lower than bars sweetened naturally. Whether to avoid it depends on your personal health priorities and sensitivity. Given that 78% of bars skip it, finding alternatives is straightforward.
What is the difference between artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols?
Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, acesulfame-K, aspartame, saccharin) are synthetic compounds that provide intense sweetness with essentially no calories. Sugar alcohols (erythritol, maltitol, xylitol, sorbitol) are derived from carbohydrates, provide some calories (roughly 0–2.4 cal/g depending on type), and can cause digestive discomfort in some people. They are completely separate categories. A bar can be free of artificial sweeteners but still contain erythritol or maltitol. This page covers only artificial sweeteners. For bars free of both, see our clean protein bars guide.
Do Quest bars have artificial sweeteners?
Yes. Every Quest bar flavor in our database contains sucralose, acesulfame potassium, or both. Quest bars are high in protein and very low in sugar, but they rely on artificial sweeteners to achieve that sweetness without adding calories. If avoiding artificial sweeteners is a priority, Quest bars are not a match. High-protein alternatives without artificial sweeteners include Gryp (25g protein), Linear (20g protein), Jacob (20g protein), and Atlas.
