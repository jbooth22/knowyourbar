What "no artificial sweeteners" actually means

Artificial sweeteners are the synthetic, zero-calorie sweeteners bar makers reach for when they want sweetness without sugar on the label. This guide screens every bar for four of them: sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, and saccharin.

19% of the 1,000+ bars in our database still have one on the label. Here is how often each one shows up, and where it usually hides.

Sucralose
248 bars19%
Usually shows up as a flavor-rounding sweetener in low-sugar or high-fiber bars, often alongside allulose or fiber syrups to hit a specific sugar number on the label.
Found in: 1st Phorm, Addra Labs, Atkins, BalanceDiet, Barebells, BSN, David, Einstein, Equate, FITCRUNCH, Fulfil, Genius Gourmet, GNC Total Lean, JiMMYBAR!, Legendary, MRE BAR, MTN OPS, Musashi, MyProtein, Neoh, NuGo, One, Power Crunch, Pure Protein, Quest, SOAR, think!, Wonderslim
Acesulfame Potassium
18 bars1%
Always paired with sucralose in the bars we track, never used on its own, adding a sweetness boost on top of the primary sweetener.
Found in: BalanceDiet, David
Aspartame
0 bars0%
Not currently used in any bar we track. Once common in diet foods generally, it has largely fallen out of favor in the protein bar category.
Saccharin
0 bars0%
Not currently used in any bar we track. The oldest artificial sweetener on this screen, now rare in this category.

Stevia and monk fruit are not on this screen

Stevia leaf extract and monk fruit (luo han guo) extract are plant-derived, not synthetic, so they don't count against a bar here. 229 of the 1,060 qualifying bars use one of them. If you want to avoid every non-sugar sweetener, including plant-derived ones, check the ingredient list yourself.

Artificial sweeteners are not the same thing as sugar alcohols either. Erythritol, maltitol, and xylitol are sugar alcohols, a different category with their own digestive tradeoffs, and they show up on a different screen. 135 bars on this page skip artificial sweeteners but still contain a sugar alcohol. If that's what you want to avoid, see our protein bars without sugar alcohols guide. 925 bars in our database have neither.

Protein bars without sucralose

Sucralose, sold under the brand name Splenda, is the artificial sweetener actually driving this guide. It is a chlorinated sugar substitute, roughly 600 times sweeter than table sugar, with zero calories and no effect on blood sugar. People skip it for different reasons: reported digestive discomfort, an aftertaste they don't like, gut microbiome research they've read, or just a preference to avoid synthetic sweeteners entirely.

248 of the 1,000+ bars we track, about 1 in 5, contain sucralose. We checked whether screening for sucralose on its own produces a different list than screening for all four artificial sweeteners this guide covers. It doesn't. Every bar in our database that contains acesulfame potassium also contains sucralose, and none contain aspartame or saccharin, so protein bars without sucralose and protein bars without artificial sweeteners are the exact same 1,060 bars across 180 brands. The Best 10 and Top 50 on this page are all sucralose-free.

It is usually a named ingredient, not a trace.
Only 110 of the 248 sucralose bars tuck it into a "contains 2% or less" clause near the end of the label. The other 138, including Pure Protein Chocolate Deluxe and FITCRUNCH Chocolate Peanut Butter, list it without that qualifier.

How we picked these

What qualifies: No sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, or saccharin anywhere in the ingredient list. Plant-derived sweeteners like stevia and monk fruit, allulose, and sugar alcohols do not count against a bar here. 1,060 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best stevia or monk fruit: Stevia or monk fruit is the only sweetener added: no allulose, no sugar alcohol, no added sugar or syrup. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars that also have no sugar alcohol, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best plant-based: Vegan-labeled bars. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
  • Highest fiber: The most fiber, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
