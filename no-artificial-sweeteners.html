What "No Artificial Sweeteners" actually means

Artificial sweeteners are widespread in protein bars. Sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame potassium (Ace-K), and saccharin are the four most common. They are calorie-free and intensely sweet, which is why manufacturers use them to hit low-sugar targets cheaply. Every bar on this list was screened for all four. None appear.

Sucralose
Most common
Found in Quest, Barebells, Pure Protein, FITCRUNCH, and most mass-market bars. Often paired with Ace-K.
Acesulfame-K
Usually paired
Almost never appears alone. Typically listed as "acesulfame potassium" alongside sucralose to enhance sweetness.
Aspartame
Less common
More common in older bar formulas and diet products. Some brands still use it, especially in protein shakes.
Saccharin
Rare in bars
Mostly phased out of protein bars but still appears in some budget and older-formula products.
What the data shows

What we found screening 983 bars

21%
of bars use artificial sweeteners
204 of 983 bars contain at least one of the four disqualifying sweeteners. Sucralose is the most common, appearing in nearly every disqualified bar. Barebells, Quest, Pure Protein, FITCRUNCH, and ONE account for the majority of failures.
Barebells is the biggest offender. 25 flavors disqualified. Every Barebells bar in the database uses sucralose and acesulfame potassium.
Quest uses sucralose in nearly all flavors. 16 flavors disqualified. A small number of newer Quest products skip it, but most do not.
Sugar alcohols are separate from this filter. 98 bars on this list still contain erythritol or another sugar alcohol. This guide only screens for artificial sweeteners, not sugar alcohols.
Whole-food brands dominate A-grades. RXBAR, Larabar, Thunderbird, and Perfect Bar all qualify and score A. Their sweeteners are dates, honey, and fruit, not lab-made compounds.
High-protein bars are harder to find here. Most bars hitting 20g+ protein rely on artificial sweeteners to keep sugar low. Off the Farm and PEAK Protein are notable exceptions.
110 brands represented. The no-artificial-sweetener pool is broad. Many smaller and whole-food brands never used them to begin with.

Best brands for No Artificial Sweeteners

Some brands have never used artificial sweeteners. Others quietly reformulated years ago. These are the ones worth knowing.

Brands to target
  • RXBAR Egg whites, dates, nuts. No artificial anything across the entire line.
  • Perfect Bar 17 flavors qualify. Sweetened with honey and fruit. A-grade ingredient quality across the board.
  • PEAK Protein High protein, A-grade, no artificial sweeteners. One of the few bars that clears every clean bar threshold.
  • Gryp (Sleeper Pick) 25g protein, under 200 calories, A-grade, no artificial sweeteners. Almost no brand recognition but the numbers are hard to argue with.
Brands to avoid
  • Barebells Every single flavor uses sucralose and acesulfame potassium. None qualify.
  • Quest 16 flavors disqualified. Sucralose appears in nearly the entire product line.
  • Pure Protein 18 flavors disqualified. Sucralose and acesulfame potassium in every formula we reviewed.

Sucralose, acesulfame potassium (Ace-K), aspartame, and saccharin are the four most common. Sucralose is by far the most widespread, appearing in most mass-market bars. Ace-K almost always appears alongside sucralose rather than on its own.

No. Sugar alcohols like erythritol, maltitol, and xylitol are a separate category. This guide only screens for the four artificial sweeteners listed above. If you also want to avoid sugar alcohols, see our No Sugar Alcohols guide.
Sucralose and Ace-K are cheap, stable, and intensely sweet. They allow manufacturers to hit low-sugar targets on nutrition labels without adding carbs or calories. For bars targeting 1-5g of sugar, artificial sweeteners are the easiest lever.
Yes, though it requires either sugar alcohols or very low-sweetness ingredients. Bars like B.T.R. Nation and Mosh hit 2-3g of sugar without artificial sweeteners by using small amounts of natural sweeteners and relying on cocoa or nut flavors for taste.
