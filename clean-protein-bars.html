What "Clean Eating Guide" actually means

Most protein bars use the word "clean" loosely. For this guide, it means something specific: an A or B ingredient quality grade from our scoring system, no artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame potassium, or saccharin), and no processed oils (canola, soybean, palm, or any hydrogenated fat). A bar has to pass all three to appear here.

Ingredient Grade
A or B
Every bar is scored ingredient by ingredient. A = clean, B = good. C through F do not qualify.
Artificial Sweeteners
Zero
Sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame potassium, and saccharin are all disqualifying. None appear in any bar on this list.
Processed Oils
None allowed
Canola, soybean, palm, and hydrogenated oils are excluded. High-oleic sunflower oil is permitted.
Protein Source
Matters
Egg whites and whey concentrate score highest. Collagen protein is flagged as a concern because it lacks essential amino acids.
What the data shows

What we found across 983 bars

65%
of bars failed the clean test
643 of 983 bars contain artificial sweeteners, processed oils, or scored C or below. Most bars marketed as clean or high-protein still rely on sucralose, canola oil, or cheap synthetic additives.
RXBAR is the most consistent A-brand. 12 flavors qualify. Short lists, egg whites as protein, dates as sweetener. No artificial anything.
Perfect Bar earns A grades at scale. 17 flavors qualify. Higher in sugar and calories than most, but the ingredient quality holds up.
Big brands mostly fail. Quest, Clif, and KIND protein bars are heavy on sucralose or processed oils. Nearly all fall below the clean threshold.
High protein and clean rarely overlap. Only 38 bars on this list hit 20g or more of protein. Most clean bars prioritize ingredient quality over protein density.
PEAK Protein is the top-scoring high-protein brand. Three flavors earn A grades with 19-20g protein, clean whey sources, and no artificial sweeteners.
Whole-food bars cluster at the top. Larabar, Thunderbird, and Bearded Bros all earn A grades. Lower protein, but ingredient lists you can actually read.

Best brands for Clean Eating Guide

Not all brands that market themselves as clean actually are. These are the ones whose ingredient lists back up the claim.

Brands to target
  • RXBAR 12 flavors qualify. Egg whites, dates, nuts. No artificial sweeteners, no oils, no filler.
  • Perfect Bar 17 flavors qualify. Real food ingredients. Higher sugar, but earns A grades across the line.
  • PEAK Protein Best if you need 20g protein and clean ingredients. Three A-grade flavors with real whey and no sucralose.
  • Gryp (Sleeper Pick) 25g protein, under 200 calories, A-grade ingredients. Almost no brand recognition but the numbers are hard to argue with.
Brands to avoid
  • Quest Uses sucralose in nearly every flavor. No Quest bar qualifies for this guide.
  • Clif Bar C-grade across the board. High sugar, processed ingredients, fortification-heavy. Not clean by any standard.
  • KIND Protein Canola oil and glucose syrup in most flavors. Some KIND bars are better, but the protein line fails the test.

Frequently asked questions

For this guide, clean means three things: an A or B ingredient quality grade, no artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame potassium, saccharin), and no processed oils (canola, soybean, palm, or hydrogenated oils). 340 of 983 bars in our database meet all three criteria.
Cleaner ingredients generally means fewer synthetic additives, more whole-food protein sources, and less dependence on ultra-processed fats and sweeteners. That said, macros still matter. A clean bar with 400 calories might not fit your goals even if the ingredient list is excellent.
Sucralose and acesulfame potassium are cheap, calorie-free, and intensely sweet. They let manufacturers hit low-sugar targets without using real sweeteners, which would add carbs and cost. Most mass-market bars use at least one.
It depends on your standard. Erythritol is a naturally occurring sugar alcohol, but commercially it is derived through fermentation of glucose, often from corn. We score it as a neutral-to-minor concern. Bars with erythritol can still earn A or B grades if the rest of the ingredient list is strong.
Egg whites, whey protein concentrate, and whole-food sources like nuts and seeds score highest. Whey isolate and pea protein score slightly lower but are still solid. Collagen protein scores as a concern because it lacks the essential amino acids needed for muscle protein synthesis.
Yes. RXBAR, Perfect Bar, PEAK Protein, and Transparent Labs all have A or B grades and consistently strong reviews. The tradeoff is usually texture: clean bars tend to be denser and less dessert-like than bars built on artificial sweeteners and cheap fillers.
