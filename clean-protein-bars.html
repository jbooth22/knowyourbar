What "clean" actually means on this page

Search for clean protein bars and most lists filter on a vibe: "real food," "no junk," a short ingredient list. We don't screen on a feeling. Clean here means three pass or fail checks stacked together: an A or B ingredient grade, no artificial sweeteners, and no processed oils. A bar has to pass all three, not just the one that's easiest to market.

Why these three checks

The two ingredient checks catch things a nutrition panel can't. Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, saccharin) show up in bars that already look great on paper, low sugar and high protein, because a synthetic sweetener is the cheapest way to hit those numbers. Processed oils (canola, soybean, palm, hydrogenated oils) do the same job for texture and shelf life. Neither shows up in the sugar or fat grams. You have to read the ingredient list to catch them.

The A or B grade stops a bar from qualifying just by skipping those two. Our grade also weighs where the protein comes from and how far down the list each ingredient sits, so a bar that skips sweeteners and seed oils but leans on a low-quality protein blend or a long list of additives still won't make it.

Four questions worth asking before you call a bar clean:

  • What's the sweetener: a natural sugar, a sugar alcohol, or one of the four synthetic ones we screen for?
  • What's the main fat: a refined seed oil, or something less processed like nut butter or coconut oil?
  • Where does the protein come from: a single quality source, or a stack of isolates and blends?
  • Does the ingredient grade back it up, or is a clean-sounding brand still grading C or below?

What clean doesn't cover

Clean and healthy aren't the same claim. None of the three checks look at macros, so a clean bar can still run high in calories or sugar. Clean also doesn't screen out sugar alcohols: 17 of the 506 bars here contain one, like erythritol or IMO. If you want to skip those too, see our protein bars without sugar alcohols guide.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

Most failing bars fail more than one check.
Of the 802 bars that fail, 544 fail two or all three checks at once, 513 of them with a processed oil and a C-or-below grade together. Only 157 fail on grade alone, 100 on processed oils alone, and 1 on artificial sweeteners alone.
Barebells and Quest fail completely.
None of the 25 Barebells flavors or 16 Quest flavors qualify. Every one contains an artificial sweetener, 34 of 41 also contain a processed oil, and 40 of 41 grade C or below.
Failing bars average more protein.
Bars that fail average 14.5g of protein against 11.3g for clean bars. Big protein numbers often come from processed protein blends and synthetic sweeteners doing the work.

How we picked these

What qualifies: An A or B ingredient grade, no artificial sweeteners (sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, saccharin), and no processed oils (canola, soybean, palm, palm kernel, sunflower and other refined seed oils, or hydrogenated fat). Sugar alcohols, stevia, monk fruit, coconut oil and high-oleic oils do not count against a bar. 506 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest net carbs: The fewest net carbs (total carbs minus fiber minus sugar alcohol), grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best non-GMO: Labeled Non-GMO by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best soy-free: Labeled soy free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
