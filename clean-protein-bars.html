Why most "clean protein bar" lists are not useful

Search "clean protein bars" and you will find list after list of the same bars, usually whatever is paying for affiliate placement that month. The word "clean" has no regulated definition, and brands use it freely on bars that contain sucralose, maltitol, palm kernel oil, and 25 other ingredients.

We took a different approach. Every bar in our database is scored based on its actual ingredient list: what ingredients appear, in what order, and what the research says about each one. A bar cannot buy its way onto this list. It either earns an A or B ingredient grade with no artificial sweeteners and no sugar alcohols, or it does not qualify.

Out of 775+ protein bars scored, 251 meet all three clean criteria. That is about 35% of the market. The other 65% contain artificial sweeteners, sugar alcohols, or both.

A note on how these scores work: We are not nutritionists or registered dieticians. We are a group of people who eat a lot of protein bars, got frustrated that "clean" meant nothing, and built a scoring system to make sense of ingredient lists. Each bar is scored by mapping its ingredients to a database of over 1,000 canonicals, each with a quality score from +4 (excellent) to -4 (harmful), weighted by ingredient position. The earlier an ingredient appears, the more it contributes to the final score. It is not a perfect system, and we are always refining it, but we think it is a meaningful and honest starting point. What the cleanest bars have in common

Looking across the top-scoring clean bars, a few patterns stand out consistently.

Protein comes first

In the highest-scoring bars, a quality protein source is the lead ingredient or appears within the first two positions. Whey protein isolate, egg whites, pea protein, nuts: these are whole or minimally processed sources that score well and put the most nutritionally significant ingredient right at the top of the list.

Sweetness comes from real food

Dates, honey, fruit, and coconut sugar appear frequently across this list. These are not zero-calorie solutions, and they do contribute natural sugars, which is why some of these bars score higher on total sugar than bars using erythritol. The difference is that these are whole food sources rather than synthetic compounds, and that distinction matters to a lot of people.

Short ingredient lists earn a bonus

Bars with fewer than eight ingredients get a small scoring bonus in our system because brevity usually reflects simplicity. That said, a longer ingredient list is not automatically worse. It depends entirely on what those ingredients are. RXBAR has six ingredients and scores exceptionally high. A bar with 20 whole-food ingredients can still score better than one with 12 ingredients that includes three synthetic sweeteners.

The real tradeoff: protein and price

The cleanest bars tend to deliver less protein than bars using isolate-heavy formulas sweetened with sucralose. RXBAR gives you 12g from egg whites and nuts. Larabar gives you 4 to 6g from dates and nuts. If 20g or more is a firm requirement, the options narrow significantly. But they exist. Atlas, Gryp, and PEAK Protein all hit 20g or more while meeting every clean criterion on this list.

Ingredients to watch out for

Artificial sweeteners

Sucralose appears in more protein bars than any other artificial sweetener, roughly 130 of the 775 bars in our database. It shows up even in bars marketed as "natural." Acesulfame potassium (also listed as Ace-K) almost always appears alongside sucralose. Aspartame is less common in bars than it once was but still present in some. All three earn a negative score in our ingredient system.

Sugar alcohols

Maltitol is the worst offender. It has a higher glycemic impact than most sugar alcohols and is known to cause significant digestive distress. It scores -4 in our system, the lowest possible rating. Erythritol is gentler on digestion but still a synthetic sugar alcohol present in over 40 bars. Xylitol and sorbitol round out the most common ones. None of them appear in any bar on this list.

Processed oils

Palm kernel oil, fractionated palm oil, and partially hydrogenated oils frequently show up as coating agents and texture ingredients. They are not in the same concern category as artificial sweeteners, but they do drag ingredient scores down when they appear early in the list. Several bars here avoid them entirely.

Frequently asked questions

What are the cleanest protein bars?
The cleanest protein bars based on ingredient quality scoring are those with no artificial sweeteners, no sugar alcohols, and whole-food ingredients in prominent positions. Top options include RXBAR, Perfect Bar, IQ Bar, Munk Pack, and Atlas. We scored 775+ bars to identify 251 that meet our clean ingredient criteria.
What protein bars have no artificial sweeteners?
Protein bars without artificial sweeteners include RXBAR, Perfect Bar, Larabar, GoMacro, IQ Bar, Simply Protein, Munk Pack, and many others. Bars to watch out for contain sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, or saccharin in the ingredient list.
Are there protein bars without sugar alcohols?
Yes. Many protein bars use whole-food sweeteners like dates, honey, or fruit instead of sugar alcohols like erythritol or maltitol. RXBAR, Larabar, Perfect Bar, IQ Bar, and Atlas bars are all examples that skip sugar alcohols entirely.
What makes a protein bar clean?
A clean protein bar typically means no artificial sweeteners, no sugar alcohols, minimal processed additives, and recognizable whole-food ingredients. Our scoring system grades bars A through F based on ingredient quality. Of 775+ bars scored, 251 earn an A or B grade with no artificial sweeteners and no sugar alcohols.
