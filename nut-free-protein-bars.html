What makes a protein bar nut free

We use the Nut Free (Y/N) label on file for every bar: the brand says the bar is free of peanuts and tree nuts. Then we read every ingredient list ourselves. 50 of 1,000+ bars, about 4%, carry the label, and none of those 50 names a peanut or a tree nut in its ingredients.

Of the 1,333 bars without the label, 712 name a tree nut (almonds, cashews, hazelnuts and the like) and 487 name peanuts. Nuts are the default protein-and-fat base for bars, which is why so few are nut free. The other 326 don't name a nut at all, but they aren't labeled either, and a label is what tells you a brand has checked for cross-contact.

What about coconut?

Coconut is a fruit. The FDA dropped it from its list of tree nuts for allergen labeling in January 2025, and most people with a tree nut allergy can eat it. 16 of the 50 nut free bars here contain coconut, so check the label if coconut is a problem for you.

We are not doctors or dietitians. These are the qualities we know people look for, so that's what we factored in. If you have a severe allergy, check the package and the brand's allergen statement every time you buy.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

Nut free is rare.
Only 50 of 1,000+ bars (4%) carry a nut free label, from 14 brands. The Feel Bar alone makes 11 of them.
Seeds do the work nuts usually do.
43 of the 50 nut free bars use seeds such as sunflower, pumpkin, chia or flax in place of nuts, and 34 use sunflower seed butter instead of a nut butter.
They grade better, with a little less protein.
62% of nut free bars grade A or B, against 48% database-wide. They average 12.3g of protein against 13.2g.

How we picked these

What qualifies: The brand labels the bar nut free (the Nut Free field in our database). We also read every ingredient list for peanuts and tree nuts, to explain why the rest miss. Coconut does not count as a tree nut. 50 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best plant-based: Vegan-labeled bars. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best gluten-free: Bars also labeled gluten free. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best soy-free: Bars also labeled soy free. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Highest fiber: The most fiber, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best on Amazon: Shown only when our Best overall pick has no Amazon listing. The same Best overall rule, limited to bars with an Amazon listing. It sits outside the numbered list, so it never takes a spot from another bar.
