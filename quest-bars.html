Are Quest Bars Healthy? We Scored All 16 Flavors

Quest bars rank in the top 3% for fiber and top 23% for protein across 1,000+ bars we've scored. Every flavor earns a B or C ingredient grade, with scores ranging from 1.7 to 6.2 and no flavor currently reaching an A. The catch: every single flavor also contains artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols, no exceptions. The data below shows exactly where Quest stands, flavor by flavor.

Quest's standout numbers are fiber and protein efficiency: averaging 12g of fiber and 11g of protein per 100 calories puts them in the top 3% and top 6% of all bars we track, respectively. The tradeoff is sugar alcohol: at 6.5g average, Quest carries more than 86% of bars in the database, which keeps sugar low but can affect digestion for some people.

What the data shows across all 16 Quest flavors

Quest's lineup is one of the most consistent we've scored. Every flavor leads with milk protein isolate and whey protein isolate, every flavor uses erythritol as its primary sweetener, and every flavor contains sucralose. The macros are predictable across the board: 18-21g protein, 1-2g sugar, 170-200 calories. So are the ingredient concerns.

Where flavors differ is in additive load. Simpler ones like Oatmeal Chocolate Chip and Double Chocolate Chunk have tighter ingredient lists and land at the top of the B range. More complex flavors like Birthday Cake, Lemon Cake, and the Dipped varieties add palm kernel oil, soluble corn fiber, and extra stabilizers, which pulls their scores down into C territory. The gap between best and worst is 4.5 points (6.2 vs. 1.7), and none of the 16 flavors currently reach an A grade.

Bottom line on Quest

Quest earns its reputation as a high-protein, low-sugar bar. Averaging 12g of fiber per bar is a genuine standout in any category, ranking in the top 3% of bars we track. But the ingredient quality grades tell a more mixed story than the marketing suggests: 9 of 16 flavors land at B, the other 7 at C, and none currently reach an A.

The limitation is just as consistent: artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols in every flavor, no exceptions. If those ingredients are a dealbreaker, Quest is not your bar regardless of which flavor you pick. If you are fine with sucralose and erythritol and you are prioritizing protein and fiber per calorie, Quest is still one of the more macro-efficient options in our database. Start with Oatmeal Chocolate Chip, Double Chocolate Chunk, or Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough. These three score highest in the lineup and have noticeably shorter ingredient lists than the rest.

Frequently asked questions about Quest bars

Quest bars score B or C on ingredient quality across all 16 flavors, with scores ranging from 1.7 to 6.2. They rank in the top 3% for fiber and top 23% for protein across 1,000+ bars we track. Every flavor contains artificial sweeteners (sucralose and stevia) and sugar alcohols (erythritol). Whether that matters depends on your dietary goals.
Oatmeal Chocolate Chip scores highest at 6.2 (B, Good), followed by Double Chocolate Chunk at 5.2 and Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough at 5.0. These flavors score highest because of shorter, simpler ingredient lists with fewer additives. The lowest scorer, Birthday Cake at 1.7 (C, Okay), adds palm kernel oil and a longer list of stabilizers.
Yes. Every Quest bar contains sucralose, and most also include stevia. There are no exceptions across the lineup. Quest uses synthetic sweeteners to keep sugar very low without relying on sugar alcohols alone. For many people this is fine. For others it's a dealbreaker, and there is no Quest flavor that avoids it.
Quest bars contain 18 to 21g of protein per bar, averaging around 20g. That puts them in the top 23% for protein across all bars in our database. The protein source is a blend of milk protein isolate and whey protein isolate, both complete animal-based proteins with strong amino acid profiles.
Yes. Every Quest bar contains erythritol, typically 5 to 9g per bar. Erythritol is generally better tolerated than other sugar alcohols, but it can still cause bloating or digestive discomfort in some people, especially in larger amounts. It's what allows Quest to keep total sugar at just 1 to 2g.
Quest ranks in the top 3% for fiber and top 23% for protein across 1,000+ bars. Every flavor scores B or C, which is a solid and consistent showing even without an A grade in the lineup. The main tradeoff versus cleaner alternatives like RXBAR or Barebells is sucralose in every flavor. Neither of those brands uses artificial sweeteners in their core bars.
Quest uses a blend of milk protein isolate and whey protein isolate. Both come from dairy, are complete proteins, and have strong amino acid profiles. This is one of the reasons Quest ingredient scores are as high as they are. Quality protein sources score well in our system, and Quest leads with them in every flavor.
