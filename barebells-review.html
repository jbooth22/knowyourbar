Are Barebells Bars Healthy? We Scored All 25 Flavors

Barebells is a strong macro bar: 20g protein and 1-2g sugar in most flavors, with a quality dairy protein blend leading the standard lineup. But every single flavor contains artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols, and the Vegan line is a different product entirely. Hydrolyzed wheat gluten as the primary protein pushes those flavors into D and F territory. Here is the full breakdown across all 25 flavors.

What the data shows across all 25 Barebells flavors

Barebells splits into two meaningfully different product lines. The standard dairy line (most flavors) leads with a protein blend of calcium caseinate, whey protein isolate, and whey protein concentrate. That is a quality protein source, and it scores well in our system. These flavors earn B and C grades. The macros are compelling: 20g protein, 1-2g sugar, 200-210 calories per bar. The tradeoffs are sucralose and maltitol in every flavor, plus glycerin in most.

The Vegan line (Hazelnut Nougat, Caramel Peanut, Fudge Brownie, Caramel Choco Chip) is a different product that scores significantly worse. Three of the four Vegan flavors use hydrolyzed wheat gluten as the primary protein source. Hydrolyzed wheat gluten is a highly processed protein derivative that scores poorly in our ingredient quality system, and it pulls those flavors into D and F grades. If you are buying Barebells specifically because of its ingredient reputation, the Vegan line does not carry the same quality as the standard dairy lineup.

The Soft Bar variants (Marshmallow Peanut Road, Salted Peanut Caramel, Minty Chocolate, Caramel Choco, Banana Caramel) have a slightly different protein composition with micellar casein isolate and whey protein hydrolysate. Some also add xylitol on top of maltitol, which is what pulls those specific flavors lower on the score chart. The core standard bars are the ones to stick with if ingredient quality is the goal.

Why the grades span B to F: two very different products

The B-to-F range is not a quirk. Barebells runs two product lines that have almost nothing in common from an ingredient standpoint, and they happen to sell under the same name.

The standard dairy bars lead with a protein blend of casein, whey isolate, and whey concentrate. That is a solid protein source, and it earns real credit in our scoring even with the maltitol and sucralose that follow. Most of these land B or C.

The vegan bars are a different story. Three of the four vegan flavors use hydrolyzed wheat gluten as their first protein ingredient. Hydrolyzed wheat gluten is a heavily processed derivative that scores poorly. On top of that, several of these bars have maltitol at position 2 and maltitol syrup at position 6, which means two separate sugar alcohol sources stacked in the top half of the ingredient list. That is what pushes them to D and F, not some rounding error.

Hazelnut Nougat is the one vegan exception. It leads with soy, pea, and rice protein isolates instead of wheat gluten, which is why it grades D rather than F.

Bottom line on Barebells

Barebells is one of the better macro bars in the market. The standard dairy lineup delivers 20g protein and 1-2g sugar in a 200-210 calorie bar, and the protein source (casein and whey isolates) is genuinely quality. If you are prioritizing protein efficiency and low sugar and you are comfortable with artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols, the core Barebells line is competitive with the best options in our database.

The Vegan line is a separate conversation. Hydrolyzed wheat gluten is not a quality protein source in our scoring system, and the D and F grades on those flavors reflect that clearly. They are not a cleaner version of the bar. They are a different product built around a lower-quality protein foundation. Start with Marshmallow Peanut Road or Peanut Butter for the best ingredient quality in the lineup, and avoid the Vegan line and any flavors containing xylitol or maltitol syrup if score matters to you.
