Brand Review

Are Barebells Bars Healthy?

Our Verdict
⚠️ Depends on which line you buy

Barebells has the widest ingredient quality score range of any major brand we track. Their dairy-protein line scores A (Clean) while their vegan line scores D (Poor). Same brand, fundamentally different products.

Both lines contain artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols. The dairy line uses high-quality whey and casein proteins. The vegan line relies on maltitol as a primary ingredient, which drives scores sharply negative.

Bottom line: Dairy line: strong. Vegan line: avoid.

Why reviewing every flavor matters

Most protein bar reviews pick one or two flavors and call the brand done. That misses the point. Within a single brand, ingredient quality can vary significantly by flavor based on protein source choices, sweetener systems, oil selections, and coating ingredients. We scored every Barebells flavor using the same objective ingredient quality scoring system we apply across our entire 672-bar database.

Barebells runs two parallel product lines: a dairy line using whey isolate, caseinate, and milk protein, and a vegan line using soy protein isolate and hydrolyzed wheat gluten. The dairy line scores well because quality dairy proteins stack significant positive points early in the ingredient list. The vegan line scores poorly because maltitol sits very high in the ingredient order, contributing a heavy negative score the protein sources cannot overcome.

Bottom line on Barebells bars

Barebells ingredient quality scores divide sharply along their dairy versus vegan lines. The dairy line earns A and B grades driven by genuinely high-quality protein sources. If you are shopping Barebells, knowing which line you are buying and choosing among the top-scoring flavors makes a significant difference.
