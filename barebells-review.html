Are Barebells Bars Healthy? We Ranked All 25 Flavors

Barebells doesn't officially split into sub-brands, but the data shows two distinct formulas hiding under one label, with different protein sources and very different ingredient quality. The core dairy-protein lineup (21 of 25 flavors) grades B or C and ranks in the top 25% for protein across 1,000+ bars we track. The 4-flavor vegan line grades D or F, the worst in the lineup, built around a lower-protein formula with heavier sweetener stacking. Sugar stays low across both lines, top 18% lowest in our database, but it's offset by a heavy dose of sugar alcohol: Barebells lands in the bottom 10% of every bar we've scored. Here is the full breakdown, flavor by flavor.

Barebells' standout numbers are protein and sugar: 18.4g of protein puts it in the top 25% of bars we track, and 1.4g of sugar puts it in the top 18% lowest. The tradeoff is sugar alcohol. At 7.6g average, Barebells carries more sugar alcohol than 90% of the bars in our database, the widest gap between a strength and a weakness of any brand we've reviewed so far.

What the data shows across all 25 Barebells flavors

Barebells doesn't market this as two sub-brands, but the split shows up clearly in the numbers, not just the vegan label. The core dairy line, 21 of 25 flavors, leads with a milk protein blend of whey protein isolate, casein, and calcium caseinate, and it delivers real protein: 16 to 20g per bar, averaging 19g. The vegan line, 4 flavors, leads with hydrolyzed wheat gluten and soy protein isolate instead, and every one of those four flavors sits flat at 15g of protein, no exceptions. That's a 21% drop in average protein between the two lines, not just an ingredient-quality gap.

The sweetener load tells the same story from a different angle. Only 1 of the 21 dairy-line flavors stacks a second sugar alcohol source (maltitol plus maltitol syrup) on top of its base sweetening. In the vegan line, 3 of 4 flavors do exactly that, and it shows: those three average 11.7g of sugar alcohol per bar against a dairy-line average of 7g. Hazlenut Nougat is the one vegan flavor that skips the second sugar alcohol source, and it's also the highest-graded of the four, not a coincidence. The pattern holds inside the vegan line and out: more sweetener stacking tracks directly with a lower grade.

Practically, this means the two lines aren't interchangeable. If you're buying Barebells for its protein numbers, the dairy line is where that reputation comes from, not the vegan line. If you need the vegan line specifically, Hazlenut Nougat is the one to reach for over the other three: it costs 10 more calories, but it also carries roughly 6g less sugar alcohol and 4g more fiber, with a shorter list of concerns.

Quality Protein Source shows up in every flavor, dairy and vegan alike, since both protein systems clear the bar for a genuine protein source, even though the grams of actual protein differ sharply between the two lines. Where flavors diverge further is Collagen Protein, present in 21 of 25 flavors (the entire dairy line) but absent from all four vegan flavors, since collagen is animal-derived. Sweetener Heavy flags 16 of 25 flavors for stacking multiple sweetening agents on top of the base maltitol, and a Long Ingredient List shows up in nearly half the lineup (12 of 25), tracking with more complex flavors like Marshmallow Peanut Road and Salted Peanut Caramel. Processed Oils is the true minority at 6 of 25, appearing mostly alongside the sugar-alcohol-heavy vegan flavors.

Bottom line on Barebells

Barebells earns its reputation as a high-protein, low-sugar option, at least for 21 of its 25 flavors. Averaging 18.4g of protein and just 1.4g of sugar per bar is a genuinely strong combination, and the core dairy line's milk protein blend is a quality source that scores well in our system. But the ingredient story has a real asterisk: sugar alcohol content lands in the bottom 10% of every bar we track, worse than 90% of the database, and that holds true across the entire lineup, dairy and vegan alike.

The bigger decision is which line you're buying. The vegan flavors, Hazlenut Nougat, Caramel Peanut, Fudge Brownie, and Caramel Choco Chip, grade D or F and are meaningfully different products from an ingredient standpoint, built around hydrolyzed wheat gluten instead of dairy protein. If you want Barebells' protein numbers without that gap, start with Marshmallow Peanut Road or Peanut Butter. Marshmallow Peanut Road leads the lineup at B grade, and Peanut Butter follows close behind, also B, both carrying the milk protein blend with the shortest list of concerns.

Which Barebells flavor should you actually buy

With a 25-flavor lineup spanning A to F, there isn't one answer to "which Barebells bar." Here's how we'd sort it depending on your goals, based on what's actually in the data above rather than the best- or worst-flavor snapshot alone.

Highest protein
Peanut Butter

Sixteen flavors tie at 20g protein, the top of the lineup. Peanut Butter grades highest of that group (B, the #2 flavor overall), so it's the one to start with if protein-per-bar is the deciding factor.

Least sugar alcohol
Caramel Cashew
Cookies & Cream
Salty Peanut

All three tie at 5g, the lowest any Barebells flavor gets. Caramel Cashew and Salty Peanut now grade B, with Cookies & Cream just behind at C, so this is less of a tradeoff against ingredient quality than it used to be, and about as good a floor for sugar alcohol as this brand offers.

Need the vegan line
Hazlenut Nougat

Still a D grade, but the only one of the four vegan flavors that skips the second sugar-alcohol source (maltitol syrup on top of maltitol), keeping it at 6g against 11 to 12g for the other three.

Skip regardless of diet
Fudge Brownie
Caramel Peanut
Caramel Choco Chip

Stack two separate sugar alcohol sources, carry the lowest protein in the lineup at 15g, and grade D or F. The only flavors that combine all three weaknesses at once.
