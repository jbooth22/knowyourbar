Are Barebells Bars Healthy? We Scored All 25 Flavors

Barebells splits into two products wearing the same label. The core dairy-protein lineup (21 of 25 flavors) grades B or C and ranks in the top 25% for protein across 1,000+ bars we track. The 4-flavor vegan line grades D or F, the worst in the lineup. Sugar stays low across the board, top 18% lowest in our database, but sugar alcohol runs high: Barebells lands in the bottom 10% of every bar we've scored. Scores range from -3.8 to 7.9. Here is the full breakdown, flavor by flavor.

Barebells' standout numbers are protein and sugar: 18.4g of protein and just 1.4g of sugar per bar put it in the top quarter and top fifth of all bars we track, respectively. The tradeoff is sugar alcohol. At 7.6g average, Barebells carries more than 90% of the bars in our database, the widest gap between a strength and a weakness of any brand we've reviewed so far.

What the data shows across all 25 Barebells flavors

Barebells is really two products sharing a wrapper design. The core dairy line, 21 of 25 flavors, leads with a milk protein blend of whey protein isolate, casein, and calcium caseinate. That is a quality protein source, and it earns real credit in our scoring even with the maltitol and sucralose that follow close behind. These flavors land B or C, with macros that are consistent across the board: 15 to 20g protein, 1 to 2g sugar, 200 to 220 calories.

The 4-flavor vegan line, Hazlenut Nougat, Caramel Peanut, Fudge Brownie, and Caramel Choco Chip, is a different story. All four swap the milk protein blend for one built mainly around hydrolyzed wheat gluten and soy protein isolate, a more processed protein source that scores poorly in our system. These are also, not coincidentally, the four lowest-scoring flavors in the entire lineup, grading D or F. They lose the Collagen Protein concern chip entirely since collagen is animal-derived, but that one improvement isn't enough to offset the rest of the formula.

The gap between best and worst is 11.7 points (7.9 vs. -3.8), the widest spread we've seen in a single-brand lineup so far. If you're buying Barebells for its protein numbers, the core dairy flavors deliver. If ingredient quality across the whole lineup matters to you, the vegan line is worth skipping.

Quality Protein Source shows up in every flavor, dairy and vegan alike, since both protein systems clear the bar for a genuine protein source. Where flavors diverge is Collagen Protein, present in 21 of 25 flavors (all of the dairy line) but absent from all four vegan flavors, and Sweetener Heavy, which flags 16 of 25 flavors for stacking multiple sweetening agents on top of the base maltitol. Processed Oils and a Long Ingredient List show up in a smaller minority and track with the more complex flavors like Marshmallow Peanut Road and Salted Peanut Caramel.

Bottom line on Barebells

Barebells earns its reputation as a high-protein, low-sugar option, at least for 21 of its 25 flavors. Averaging 18.4g of protein and just 1.4g of sugar per bar is a genuinely strong combination, and the core dairy line's milk protein blend is a quality source that scores well in our system. But the ingredient story has a real asterisk: sugar alcohol content lands in the bottom 10% of every bar we track, worse than 90% of the database, and that holds true across the entire lineup, dairy and vegan alike.

The bigger decision is which line you're buying. The vegan flavors, Hazlenut Nougat, Caramel Peanut, Fudge Brownie, and Caramel Choco Chip, grade D or F and are meaningfully different products from an ingredient standpoint, built around hydrolyzed wheat gluten instead of dairy protein. If you want Barebells' protein numbers without that gap, start with Marshmallow Peanut Road or Peanut Butter. Both lead the lineup at B grade and carry the milk protein blend with the shortest list of concerns.
