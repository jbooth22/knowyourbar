The 10 Best Protein Bars Without Seed Oils

Seed oils are refined oils pressed from seeds, like canola, soybean, sunflower and palm kernel. Protein bars use them in coatings, crisps and fillings because they are cheap and shelf-stable. Some people avoid them for how heavily they are processed or for their omega-6 content. We screen every bar for thirteen of them.

Of the 1,000+ bars we track, 695 have none anywhere in the ingredient list. The other 47% do, most often palm kernel oil. High-oleic oils, coconut oil and nut butters don't count against a bar here.

What "no seed oils" actually means

Seed and vegetable oils are some of the most common additives in processed food, and protein bars are no exception. This guide screens every bar for thirteen of them: canola, rapeseed, soybean, palm, palm kernel, palm fruit, sunflower, safflower, cottonseed, corn, grapeseed, and rice bran oil, plus any bar listing generic "vegetable oil" or "hydrogenated"/"partially hydrogenated" fat. High-oleic sunflower and safflower oil are permitted, since their fatty acid profile runs closer to olive oil than to the standard refined version of the same seed.

47% of the 1,000+ bars in our database still have a seed oil on the label. Here is how often each one shows up, and where it usually hides.

What still counts as no seed oil

Coconut oil comes from coconut flesh, not a seed, so it doesn't count against a bar here. 164 of the 695 bars on this page use coconut oil or coconut butter, and 8 use a high-oleic oil. Nut and seed butters, like peanut, almond or sunflower butter, are whole foods, not refined oils, so they don't count either.

Do Pure Protein, Perfect Bar, Built Bar, and IQBAR use seed oils?

Pure Protein and Built Bar use a seed or vegetable oil in every flavor we checked. Perfect Bar and IQBAR use none. Here is exactly which oils each brand lists, pulled straight from the ingredient label of every flavor in our database. Every other big brand is in the big brands table below.

Built Bar: 13 of 13 flavors list palm kernel oil, and two other oils also show up on some flavors: palm oil (8), sunflower oil (1). No flavor clears this guide.

IQBAR: None of the 10 flavors use canola oil, sunflower oil, or any other seed or vegetable oil. The fat comes from nuts, seeds, and nut butters used as whole-food ingredients, not an added refined oil.

How we picked these

What qualifies: No canola, rapeseed, soybean, palm, palm kernel, palm fruit, sunflower, safflower, cottonseed, corn, grapeseed, rice bran or generic vegetable oil, and no hydrogenated fat, anywhere in the ingredient list. High-oleic sunflower and safflower oil, coconut oil, and nut or seed butters do not count against a bar. 695 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • No added oil at all: No oil, fat, shortening or MCT of any kind on the label, seed or otherwise. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best date-sweetened: Dates on the label, and no added sugar, syrup or sugar substitute. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best snack size: Under 150 calories. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
