What counts as a seed oil?

The four seed oils found in protein bars

Seed oils are extracted from plant seeds using industrial processes including pressing, solvent extraction, and high-heat refining. They are high in polyunsaturated fatty acids, particularly omega-6s. In protein bars they appear in coatings, fillings, and as fat sources. These are the only four seed oils that appear in our database of 898 bars.

Sunflower Oil
The most common seed oil in protein bars. High in omega-6 linoleic acid. Standard sunflower oil is highly refined. Note: high oleic sunflower oil has a different fatty acid profile and is not flagged by this page's filter.
Found in 181 of 898 bars
Soybean Oil
High omega-6 content. One of the most widely used cooking oils globally. Often partially hydrogenated in older formulations, though modern bars typically use fully refined soybean oil.
Found in 64 of 898 bars
Canola Oil
Derived from rapeseed. Moderate omega-6 content relative to sunflower and soybean. Refined through high-heat and solvent processes. Used as a neutral fat source in some bar coatings and fillings.
Found in 58 of 898 bars
Safflower Oil
Among the highest omega-6 content of any oil. Used in some bar formulations as a fat source. Less common than sunflower or canola but present in a small number of bars.
Found in 11 of 898 bars
Not seed oils: Coconut oil, palm oil, olive oil, and avocado oil come from fruit flesh, not seeds, and are not included in this filter. High oleic sunflower oil has a modified fatty acid profile closer to olive oil and is treated separately. Cocoa butter is also not a seed oil.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What are seed oils in protein bars?
Seed oils are vegetable oils extracted from the seeds of plants, primarily through industrial processing. The seed oils most commonly found in protein bars are sunflower oil (181 bars), soybean oil (64 bars), canola oil (58 bars), and safflower oil (11 bars). They are used as fat sources in coatings, fillings, and to improve texture. Some people prefer to avoid them due to their high omega-6 fatty acid content and the processing methods involved in their production.
Which protein bars have no seed oils?
Protein bars without seed oils include RXBAR, Larabar, Perfect Bar, Kize, Linear, Gryp, Transparent Labs, 1st Phorm, and Healthy Eating on the Go, among many others. Of the 898 bars we scored, 617 (69%) contain no seed oils. Brands like RXBAR, Perfect Bar, and Larabar avoid seed oils across their entire lineup.
Are seed oils in protein bars bad for you?
The health effects of seed oils are genuinely debated. They are high in omega-6 fatty acids, and some researchers argue that excessive omega-6 consumption relative to omega-3 may promote inflammation. Others point out that the amounts in a single protein bar are small and that refined seed oils have a long safety record. Our scoring system does not penalize seed oils as heavily as artificial sweeteners — we mark their presence so you can make your own call. 69% of bars in our database skip them entirely.
What is the difference between seed oils and other vegetable oils?
Seed oils specifically come from seeds: sunflower seeds, soybeans, canola (rapeseed), safflower seeds, corn kernels. Other plant oils like coconut oil, olive oil, and palm oil come from the fruit flesh rather than seeds and have a different fatty acid profile. Coconut oil and olive oil are not seed oils. High oleic sunflower oil is a modified version of sunflower oil with a different fatty acid composition — our scoring treats it as separate from standard sunflower oil.
Do Quest bars have seed oils?
Quest bars do not contain sunflower, canola, or soybean oil in most flavors. They do contain palm kernel oil in many flavors, which is not a seed oil — it comes from the palm fruit. Quest bars also contain sucralose and other artificial sweeteners in every flavor. If avoiding seed oils specifically is your goal, many Quest flavors qualify, but if you're also avoiding artificial sweeteners, Quest is not a match.
