How we screened 1,181 bars for GLP-1

People taking GLP-1 medications usually deal with a smaller appetite and less food volume overall, so the bar itself needs to carry more nutritional weight per bite. We started with protein density: 15g or more protein at 200 calories or less. We added a low sugar ceiling and a fiber minimum for satiety, then excluded sugar alcohol entirely, since GI tolerance is a common concern on these medications and sugar alcohols are a frequent trigger for bloating and digestive discomfort. Finally we required an A or B ingredient quality grade so the list is not just a macro spreadsheet. 28 of 1,181 bars, 2.4% of the database, clear all six.

Is your brand good for GLP-1?

We are not doctors or dietitians, and this is not medical advice. What follows is our own read of the numbers: for every brand we track, we checked what share of its flavors clear the same six-criteria screen used above, then averaged the protein, calories, and sugar across its whole lineup. A brand can average well and still have one bad flavor, or average poorly and still have one good one, so the rating below is a starting point for your own label-reading, not a verdict.

Likely means half or more of the brand's tracked flavors clear our full screen. Mixed means some flavors clear it and some do not, so the specific flavor matters. Less Likely means none of the brand's tracked flavors currently clear it, usually on sugar, protein, or sugar alcohol rather than any one thing. Because this is the tightest screen on the site, most brands land in Less Likely.

Frequently asked questions

We are not doctors, but here is what we filtered on: enough protein to matter, a calorie count that fits a smaller appetite, low sugar, enough fiber for satiety, zero sugar alcohol, and an A or B ingredient quality grade. GLP-1 medications reduce how much food you can comfortably eat, so every bite needs to carry more protein per calorie than a typical bar. We required 15g or more protein, 200 calories or less, 4g or less sugar, 3g or more fiber, no sugar alcohol at all, and an A or B grade for every bar on this list.
Reduced appetite means less total food volume, so each bite has to work harder nutritionally. A bar with 15g of protein at 200 calories or less delivers meaningfully more protein per calorie than a typical 20g-protein, 300-calorie bar. Qualifying bars on this list average 15.7g of protein in a much smaller calorie budget.
GI tolerance is a common concern on GLP-1 medications, and sugar alcohols are a frequent trigger for bloating, gas, and digestive discomfort, on top of whatever GI side effects the medication itself may already cause. Unlike our keto and diabetics guides, which allow most sugar alcohols and only exclude the maltitol family, this list excludes sugar alcohol entirely. The goal here is not blood sugar math, it is minimizing a variable that commonly causes stomach upset for people already managing GI side effects.
A calorie ceiling keeps the list focused on bars that fit a smaller-appetite eating pattern rather than dense, calorie-heavy bars that happen to also have a lot of protein. A 45g-protein bar at 330 calories is a fine bar, but it does not fit the volume constraint this list is built around. We capped calories at 200 so every bar on this list is genuinely appropriate for smaller, more frequent eating.
Fiber supports satiety and slows digestion, which matters when appetite and food volume are already reduced. We required at least 3g of fiber for every bar on this list.
Our A through F grade rates how clean and minimally processed a bar's ingredients are. It is a separate measurement from GLP-1 suitability. We required an A or B grade for every bar on this list so you are not trading one health goal for a heavily processed ingredient list.
Out of 1,181 bars in our database, 28 meet all six criteria: 15g or more protein, 200 calories or less, 4g or less sugar, 3g or more fiber, no sugar alcohol, and an A or B ingredient grade. That is about 2.4% of the full database, the tightest screen of any guide on this site.
