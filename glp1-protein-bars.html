The 10 Best Protein Bars for GLP-1

GLP-1 medications shrink your appetite, so a snack has to deliver real protein in a small, easy-to-tolerate package. We screened every bar for six things people on these medications look for: 15g+ protein, 200 calories or less, 4g or less sugar, 3g+ fiber, no sugar alcohols, and an A or B ingredient grade.

Of the 1,000+ bars we track, only 15 pass all six, about 1%. 140 bars hit the protein, calorie, sugar and fiber numbers, but most of those use a sugar alcohol or grade C or below. We are not doctors or dietitians. These are the qualities we know people look for, so that's what we factored in.

What "GLP-1 friendly" means on this page

People on GLP-1 medications usually care about three things first: enough protein to make a small amount of food count, calories low enough to fit a reduced appetite without crowding out real meals, and low sugar. We added three more checks on top: at least 3g of fiber for fullness, no sugar alcohols at all (GI tolerance is a common concern on these medications), and an A or B ingredient grade.

15 of 1,000+ bars pass all six. Here is how often the checks catch a bar.

Why no sugar alcohols at all

Our keto and diabetics guides allow low-glycemic sugar alcohols like erythritol and only exclude maltitol. This guide is stricter because the question here is digestive comfort, not just blood sugar. We check the ingredient list as well as the gram line, since many labels name a sugar alcohol but declare 0g. IMO (isomalto-oligosaccharides) counts too.

Why protein per calorie matters here

Protein per 100 calories lets you compare bars of different sizes on equal footing. A bar with 20g of protein at 250 calories is less efficient than one with 15g at 150 calories, even though the first has more total protein. Bars here average 10g of protein per 100 calories and 187 calories.

Everyone on these medications has different tolerances. Everything on this page is a starting filter built from label data, not a substitute for a conversation with your doctor or a registered dietitian about what fits your treatment plan.

How we picked these

What qualifies: 15g or more protein, 200 calories or less, 4g or less sugar, 3g or more fiber, no sugar alcohol (0g on the label and none named in the ingredients, IMO included), and an A or B ingredient grade. 15 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
  • Highest fiber: The most fiber, grade B or better, 15g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar, grade B or better, 15g+ protein (no bar here has a sugar alcohol).
  • Best plant-based: Labeled vegan by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
