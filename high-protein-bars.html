What counts as a high protein bar

We draw the line at 20g of protein per bar. Most bars don't get there: the middle bar in our database has 13g. At 20g a bar carries about as much protein as a small meal, which is what most people mean when they shop for a high protein bar.

Getting to 20g usually takes a concentrated protein (whey, milk protein, soy) and something to make it taste good without sugar. That is where these bars run into trouble. Here is how often the 301 high protein bars carry the things people try to avoid.

Where the protein comes from

The first protein named on the label, across the 301 bars with 20g or more:

  • Milk protein or casein: 112 bars
  • Whey: 63 bars
  • Soy protein: 42 bars
  • Nuts or nut butter: 34 bars
  • Collagen: 22 bars
  • Pea protein: 13 bars
  • Rice protein: 13 bars
  • Egg whites: 2 bars

Whey, milk protein, soy and egg whites are complete proteins. Pea and rice protein are often paired to round each other out. Collagen is the exception: it raises the protein number but lacks tryptophan, an essential amino acid, so our scoring rates it the weakest of the positive protein sources. Whey isolate and egg whites rate highest.

High protein doesn't screen on ingredient grade

This list is about one number, so 216 of the 301 bars that qualify grade C or below. Every bar in the Best 10 grades A or B, and the list further down puts A and B bars first.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

More protein, lower grades.
Only 28% of bars with 20g+ protein grade A or B, against 46% of the whole database. 45% use an artificial sweetener (19% across all bars) and 65% a sugar alcohol.
The extra protein brings extra calories.
Bars with 20g+ protein average 240 calories, against 199 for the rest. 89 of the 301 are over 250 calories.
The biggest numbers come with the worst grades.
The most protein in any bar we track is 46g (Musashi Chocolate and Cookies and Cream, 320 calories, grade D). All 18 bars with 30g or more grade C or below. The most protein in an A or B bar is 28g.

How we picked these

What qualifies: 20g or more protein per bar. No grade check on the list itself. 301 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 20g+ protein.
  • Best plant-based: Labeled vegan by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 20g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol (none in the ingredients and 0g on the label), grade B or better.
  • Best gluten-free: Labeled gluten free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 20g+ protein.
