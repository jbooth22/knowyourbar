What actually pushes a bar past 11g of fiber

Fiber on a protein bar label comes from one of two places: whole-food ingredients (nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains) or an isolated fiber added to raise the number. Of the 103 bars that clear our 11g Extreme Fiber cutoff, 90% contain at least one of the five added fibers below, often two or three stacked together. None of these are red flags on their own; they're standard ways to add fiber. Two of our Best 10 picks (Daryl's Bars Frosted Vanilla Cupcake and Daryl's Bars Chocolate Hazelnut) get part of their fiber from IMO.

Tapioca fiber
37 bars36%
A resistant-starch fiber made from cassava root, added to boost the fiber count without changing texture much.
Found in: 1st Tee, Atlas, Elavi, Floura, good! Snacks, No Cow, Real Food Bar, Trubar, Tusol, War On Sugar
Soluble corn fiber
27 bars26%
A corn-derived resistant dextrin used the same way as tapioca or chicory fiber, mainly to raise the fiber line.
Found in: Atkins, BalanceDiet, Love Good, MyProtein, No Cow, Quest, Redefine
Polydextrose
25 bars24%
A synthetic soluble fiber used as a bulking agent, common in bars that also use a sugar alcohol.
Found in: 1st Tee, Atkins, BalanceDiet, MyProtein, Quest, think!
Chicory root / inulin
24 bars23%
A soluble fiber extracted from chicory root, one of the most common added-fiber sources.
Found in: 1st Tee, BalanceDiet, Floura, form, Love Good, NuGo, Redefine, Santa Cruz Paleo

13 bars13%
A syrup sold as a prebiotic fiber. Not the sugar alcohol isomalt despite the similar name. Some research suggests the body digests much of it like a sugar.

The three tiers of high fiber

11g is a real jump, and not every bar needs to clear it. The FDA's labeling rule sets 5g as the cutoff for an "excellent source of fiber" claim, and most people looking for a higher-fiber bar are better served starting there. We track three cumulative cutoffs on the same number: every Extreme Fiber bar also counts as Very High and High Fiber.

  • High Fiber, 5g+: 613 bars (47% of the database), 128 brands. A good example that doesn't reach 8g: Gryp Peanut Butter Choco Chunk (A, 6g fiber, 25g protein).
  • Very High Fiber, 8g+: 243 bars (19%), 62 brands. A good example that doesn't reach 11g: Rello Vanilla Crisp (A, 9g fiber, 20g protein).
  • Extreme Fiber, 11g+: 103 bars (8%), 24 brands. The tier this page ranks.

Fiber above roughly 10 to 15g in one sitting can cause bloating if you're not used to it, especially from added fibers like polydextrose or IMO. If you're new to high-fiber bars, the 5g or 8g tier is a gentler start.

How we picked these

What qualifies: 11g or more of dietary fiber per bar, straight off the nutrition panel (our Extreme Fiber tier). No macro or ingredient-grade gate beyond that. 103 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify, 41 of them with an A or B grade.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.
  • No bar from a national grocery or big-box brand qualified for the "Best from a big brand" spot that wasn't already picked, so Most fiber per calorie takes its place.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Highest fiber: The most fiber, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest net carbs: The fewest net carbs (total carbs minus fiber minus sugar alcohol), grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Best plant-based: Labeled vegan by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
  • Most fiber per calorie: The most fiber per 100 calories, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
