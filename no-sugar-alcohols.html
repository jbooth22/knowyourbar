What counts as a sugar alcohol?

The five sugar alcohols found in protein bars

Sugar alcohols are neither sugars nor alcohols — the name comes from their chemical structure. They provide sweetness with fewer calories than sugar (typically 0–2.4 cal/g vs sugar's 4 cal/g) because they are not fully absorbed. That partial absorption is also what causes digestive issues in some people. These are the only five that appear in protein bars in our database.

Maltitol
The most common sugar alcohol in bars and the most likely to cause digestive discomfort. Has a high glycemic impact compared to other sugar alcohols — roughly half of sugar's glycemic index. Every Barebells flavor contains maltitol.
Found in 162 of 898 bars
Erythritol
Better tolerated than maltitol for most people. Mostly absorbed before reaching the large intestine. Near-zero glycemic impact. Quest bars use erythritol to achieve very low sugar counts. Recent research has raised questions about cardiovascular effects at high intake.
Found in 67 of 898 bars
Isomalt
Derived from beet sugar. Less sweet than sugar, often used in chocolate coatings. Moderate digestive tolerance — better than maltitol, worse than erythritol. Common in European-style confectionery bars.
Found in 47 of 898 bars
Sorbitol
One of the more problematic sugar alcohols for digestive symptoms at moderate quantities. Has a well-documented laxative effect. Less common in modern bars than it was a decade ago.
Found in 25 of 898 bars
Not a sugar alcohol: Glycerin (also called glycerol) appears on many bar labels and is sometimes confused with sugar alcohols. It is a separate compound and is not included in this filter. Stevia, monk fruit, and allulose are also not sugar alcohols.

640 of 894 scored bars are free of maltitol, erythritol, isomalt, sorbitol, and xylitol. Maltitol alone accounts for 162 bars — more than erythritol, isomalt, and sorbitol combined.
Maltitol is the main one to watch for. 162 bars contain maltitol — it is in every single Barebells flavor, most No Cow flavors, and many other popular brands. It has the highest glycemic impact of any common sugar alcohol and is most likely to cause digestive symptoms.
Quest uses erythritol, not maltitol. Quest bars achieve very low sugar counts through erythritol, which is better tolerated than maltitol. But if you want to avoid all sugar alcohols, Quest still does not qualify.
Some whole brands are entirely clean. RXBAR, Larabar, Perfect Bar, GoMacro, Bearded Bros, and Jonesbar have zero sugar alcohols across their entire lineup. Sweetness in these bars comes from dates, honey, or fruit.

High protein without sugar alcohols is achievable. Gryp hits 25g protein with no sugar alcohols. Linear and PEAK Protein both hit 20g. The assumption that high-protein bars require sugar alcohols is not supported by the data.
Sugar alcohols and artificial sweeteners often appear together. Many bars use both — erythritol for bulk sweetness and sucralose for intensity. If you want to avoid both categories, our clean protein bars guide covers that stricter definition.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

What are sugar alcohols in protein bars?
Sugar alcohols are carbohydrate-derived sweeteners that provide sweetness with fewer calories than sugar. They are not fully absorbed by the body, which reduces their caloric impact but can cause digestive discomfort — bloating, gas, and diarrhea — in sensitive individuals. The five sugar alcohols found in protein bars are maltitol (162 bars), erythritol (67 bars), isomalt (47 bars), sorbitol (25 bars), and xylitol (6 bars). Maltitol is the most problematic for digestive symptoms. Erythritol is generally better tolerated.
What protein bars have no sugar alcohols?
Protein bars with no sugar alcohols include RXBAR, Larabar, Perfect Bar, Kize, Linear, Gryp, Transparent Labs, GoMacro, Bearded Bros, Jonesbar, and many others. Of the 898 bars we scored, 640 (71%) contain no sugar alcohols. Entire brands like RXBAR, Larabar, and Perfect Bar avoid sugar alcohols across every flavor.
Is erythritol in protein bars bad for you?
Erythritol is generally better tolerated than other sugar alcohols because it is mostly absorbed in the small intestine rather than fermented in the large intestine. At typical protein bar quantities, most people do not experience digestive issues. However, a 2023 study raised questions about a potential link between high erythritol blood levels and cardiovascular risk — though this is not yet established causation. Our scoring system rates erythritol as a concern ingredient. Whether to avoid it is a personal decision based on your health goals.
What is the difference between sugar alcohols and artificial sweeteners?
They are completely different categories. Sugar alcohols (erythritol, maltitol, xylitol, sorbitol, isomalt) are derived from carbohydrates and provide some calories. Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, saccharin) are synthetic compounds that provide no calories. A bar can contain sugar alcohols but no artificial sweeteners, or vice versa. This page covers sugar alcohols only. See our no artificial sweeteners guide for the other category.
Do Quest bars have sugar alcohols?
Yes. Quest bars contain erythritol in most flavors. This is what gives them their very low sugar count — erythritol provides sweetness without registering as sugar. Quest bars do not contain maltitol. If erythritol specifically is what you want to avoid, Quest bars are not a match. High-protein alternatives without any sugar alcohols include Linear (20g protein), Gryp (25g protein), and PEAK Protein (20g protein).
