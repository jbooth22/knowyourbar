The 6 Best Protein Bars with Creatine

Creatine is one of the most studied supplements for strength training, and a few brands now put it in a protein bar. It only does much at the dose research uses, 3 to 5 grams a day, so the number on the label matters as much as the protein.

Only 14 of the 1,000+ bars we track list creatine, from 6 brands. 9 carry a full 3g to 5g; 5 carry a trace 1g to 1.2g. Just 6 have an A or B ingredient grade and 10g+ protein, so our list has 6 picks, not 10.

Two dose tiers, not one creatine category

Most creatine research on strength and training uses 3 to 5 grams a day, taken consistently. The bars on this page don't spread evenly around that number; they fall into two groups. One carries a full daily dose. The other carries a gram or so, well under what studies use.

Dose and ingredient quality don't line up here. The only A-grade creatine bars, from Rello, carry 1.2g. If you want a full dose from a bar, look at the clinical tier and check the grade. If you already take creatine powder, the dose in a bar matters less.

The form matters more than the delivery. All 12 bars that name their creatine in the ingredients use creatine monohydrate, the same form used in most creatine research and sold as powder.

How we picked these

What qualifies: Any creatine listed on the label, no minimum dose. No macro or ingredient-grade gate beyond that. 14 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify, 6 of them with an A or B grade.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the list, and no brand gets more than 2 spots.
  • This list has 6 picks, not 10. Only a few brands make these bars, and with at most 2 per brand and the grade and protein rules above, 6 is as many as qualify. We don't lower the bar to fill 10 spots.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.
  • No national grocery or big-box brand makes a creatine bar, so there is no "Best from a big brand" pick.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Highest creatine dose: The most creatine per bar, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.

How the A to F grades themselves are calculated: our scoring methodology. Who built this and why: About Know Your Bar.
