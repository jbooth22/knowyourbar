Protein Bars with Creatine - We Screened 1,307 Bars, 14 Had Any

We pulled every bar in our 1,307-bar database with a declared creatine amount, no minimum required. Only 14 qualify, across just 6 brands, small enough that this comes down to individual formulas, not a genre. The doses split into two real tiers: 9 bars from 4 brands carry a clinically dosed 3g or 5g, in line with the maintenance range most creatine research studies, while 5 bars from 2 brands carry a trace 1g to 1.2g, well under that range. We ranked all 14 by ingredient quality, and dose does not predict the winner: the two best-scoring bars on this page carry the smallest declared amount, not the largest.

Two dose tiers, not one creatine category

Every bar on this page states a creatine amount on the label, and those amounts cluster into two distinct tiers rather than a smooth range. We split all 14 bars accordingly: a clinical tier matching the 3 to 5 gram maintenance dose most creatine research studies, and a trace tier well under it. The clinical tier is the bigger of the two, but it is not the better-scoring one.

The 6 brands making creatine bars right now

This category is small enough, 14 bars from 6 brands, that a Consider/Mixed/Avoid table would mostly be telling you about single flavors dressed up as a brand verdict. Here's the honest rundown instead. Grades reflect ingredient quality only, not a creatine-specific rating.

Rello makes 2 flavors, both a trace 1.2g dose, and both grade A, the cleanest lineup on this page by a wide margin. Its creatine comes from a branded monohydrate (Creativalis), alongside a whey and milk protein blend.

JiMMYBAR! is the largest lineup here at 4 flavors, all at a full clinical 5g dose, and the least consistent: grades run B, B, C, and F within the same brand at the identical dose. Strawberry and Blueberry Lemon are the pick if you want this brand; Double Fudge Brownie is the one to skip.

PROGO makes 3 flavors at the gentlest dose on the page, 1g, and grades B, B, and C. Its clean pick, Blueberry Crunch, pairs 19g of protein with just 180 calories.

Daily Bar makes 2 flavors at a clinical 3g dose, both grading C, with cane sugar and glycerin among the concern ingredients. Its Chocolate Peanut Butter Banger carries 10g of fiber, the most of any bar on this page.

SOAR makes 2 flavors at a full clinical 5g dose, both grading C. Both lean on maltitol syrup and erythritol rather than cutting sugar outright, worth knowing if "1g of sugar" is what drew you in.

Fields Good makes 1 flavor, Mocha Chocolate, at a clinical 3g dose, grading C. It's built around creatine and focus rather than a protein number: just 3g of protein, far below every other bar on this page.

Frequently asked questions

It varies a lot, and that's the whole story on this page. Of the 14 bars in our database that declare creatine, 5 (from Rello and PROGO) carry a trace 1g to 1.2g, and 9 (from Daily Bar, JiMMYBAR!, SOAR, and Fields Good) carry a clinically dosed 3g or 5g, in line with the maintenance range most creatine research actually studies. Check the dose column before assuming a bar with a creatine badge gives you a meaningful amount.
Probably not much on its own. Most creatine research on strength, power, and muscle performance uses a maintenance dose of 3 to 5 grams per day, or a loading phase well above that. A 1g dose, what 5 of the 14 bars on this page declare, is well under that range. It's not nothing, but it's closer to a token amount than a functional one.
The sports-nutrition research most commonly cites 3 to 5 grams per day for maintenance, taken consistently, with some protocols using a short loading phase of up to 20g per day split into smaller doses. 9 of the 14 bars on this page land in that 3 to 5 gram range. The form of creatine matters more than the delivery vehicle. Every bar on this page that names its source lists creatine monohydrate, the same form used in most creatine research and the powder aisle. Rello uses a branded version, Creativalis, which is still creatine monohydrate. A bar just bundles the dose into a snack instead of a scoop.
Creatine is a deliberate supplement-style formulation choice aimed at a training-focused customer, not a byproduct of a typical bar recipe.
