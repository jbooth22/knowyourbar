Four caffeine zones, measured against a cup of coffee

Every bar on this page states its caffeine content on the label. We grouped all 41 into four zones based on how that dose compares to a standard 8oz cup of brewed coffee, which runs roughly 95mg. Most bars sit in light-to-moderate territory, but a small handful now reach well past a typical energy drink threshold.

What the data shows

What we found filtering 1,028 bars for caffeine

96%
of protein bars carry zero caffeine
Only 41 of 1,028 bars in our database declare any caffeine at all. This is a genuinely small, deliberate subcategory, not a spectrum most brands dabble in.
The highest-caffeine bars are also the worst-formulated. All 3 bars in our Very High zone (150mg+) come from Jesse's WAKEUP! and all 3 score F, weighed down by hydrogenated oils, artificial colors, and sugar-forward coatings. More caffeine did not buy better ingredients here.
The High zone (90-149mg) is the sweet spot for quality. Only 7 bars qualify, all from Quantum and Battle Bars, but 6 of 7 score B or better. If you want a genuinely strong dose without a quality tradeoff, this is currently the zone to shop.
Verb still owns the Moderate zone almost entirely. 11 of 21 bars in the 50-89mg range are Verb, and most land in C territory thanks to a sweetener-heavy formula flagged across nearly every flavor.
JiMMYBAR! brings creatine and caffeine together, but ingredient quality lags. Its 7 caffeinated flavors average a -2.2 score, the second-worst brand average in this set, mostly on Maltitol and hydrogenated oils.
Unhinged Chocolate and Coffee remains the standout. It's the highest-scoring caffeinated bar in the entire database at 10.9, and the only A-grade bar under 80mg of caffeine.
A single Jesse's WAKEUP! bar can eat most of your daily caffeine budget. At 350mg, the Dark Chocolate and Vanilla Creme flavors each carry roughly 87% of the FDA's commonly cited 400mg/day guidance for healthy adults, in one bar, before counting your morning coffee.

Brands worth knowing for caffeine

Caffeine content and ingredient quality are two separate questions, and this category makes that obvious. Some brands treat caffeine as one more whole-food ingredient. Others just add it to an otherwise average, or worse, bar. Here's how the 14 brands in this dataset split.

Worth prioritizing
  • Unhinged Only one flavor, but it's the single best-scoring caffeinated bar in the entire database.
  • Supervibe Three flavors at 80mg, all B or better, two of them A-grade.
  • The Feel Bar One flavor, B-grade, a genuinely whole-food-forward formula at 65mg.
  • Quantum Six flavors at 100mg, the strongest dose without a quality drop, 5 of 6 score B or better.
Proceed with caution
  • Jesse's WAKEUP! All 3 flavors grade F, and all 3 carry 175-350mg of caffeine, the highest doses on this page paired with the worst formulas.
  • JiMMYBAR! 7 caffeinated flavors, none score above C, dragged down by Maltitol and hydrogenated oils despite the creatine angle.
  • Verb The largest lineup at 11 flavors, all 80mg, but nearly every flavor carries a sweetener-heavy flag and averages a C grade.
  • Clif Builders One flavor, F grade, among the lowest-scoring bars in the caffeine set.

Frequently asked questions

An average brewed 8oz cup of coffee runs roughly 95mg of caffeine. Bars on this page range from 23mg, a fraction of a cup, up to 350mg, more than three cups or close to a strong energy drink. Most fall in the light-to-moderate range, but a few outliers run much higher, so check the zone before you buy.
80mg sits close to a standard cup of drip coffee. For comparison, a shot of espresso is roughly 60-65mg and many energy drinks run 150-300mg. Most bars in our database that carry caffeine land in this same light-to-moderate range rather than energy-drink territory, though a small handful now reach 175-350mg.
Some bars source caffeine from actual coffee, guarana seed, or green tea extract, which arrives along with other plant compounds. Others list plain caffeine (sometimes as caffeine anhydrous) added directly to the formula. Both raise the same total caffeine number on the label, but the ingredient list tells you which route a bar took, and that shows up in our ingredient scoring.
That depends on your total daily caffeine intake from everything else you consume, and on your personal sensitivity. The FDA generally cites 400mg per day as a reasonable upper limit for healthy adults. A light or moderate bar is a small fraction of that, but the handful of bars on this page running 175-350mg can approach or exceed that limit on their own, especially stacked on top of your usual coffee or tea.
Caffeine is a narrow, deliberate formulation choice, not a byproduct of other ingredients, so a brand has to build a bar around it on purpose. Most major brands like Quest, Barebells, and Clif's core lineup skip it entirely and let people pair a regular bar with their own coffee instead.
