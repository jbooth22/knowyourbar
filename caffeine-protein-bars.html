Where the caffeine comes from

Some bars get their caffeine from real coffee, espresso, matcha or tea in the recipe, which brings other plant compounds along with it. Others add caffeine directly, listed as "caffeine" (often "green tea caffeine" or "caffeine from green coffee"). Both count the same on the label; the ingredient list tells you which route a bar took. Of the 42 bars here, 27 add caffeine directly and 12 get it from coffee or tea alone. 3 list caffeine on the nutrition panel without naming a source in the ingredients.

The FDA cites 400mg a day as an amount not generally linked to negative effects in healthy adults. A light or moderate bar is a small part of that; a Very High bar can be most of it on its own, before your morning coffee.

Heads up: We are not doctors or dietitians, and this page is not medical advice. Talk to your doctor if you have questions about caffeine and your health, especially if you are pregnant, sensitive to stimulants, or stack these with other caffeinated drinks.

How we picked these

What qualifies: Any caffeine listed on the label, no minimum dose. No macro or ingredient-grade gate beyond that. 42 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify, 24 of them with an A or B grade.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the list, and no brand gets more than 2 spots.
  • This list has 7 picks, not 10. Only a few brands make these bars, and with at most 2 per brand and the grade and protein rules above, 7 is as many as qualify. We don't lower the bar to fill 10 spots.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.
  • No caffeinated bar from a national grocery or big-box brand has an A or B grade and 10g+ protein, so there is no "Best from a big brand" pick.
  • Cleanest ingredients and Most protein per calorie had no eligible bar left once the other picks were made, so they are not on the list.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Most caffeine: The most caffeine per bar, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
  • Closest to a cup of coffee: 50 to 94mg of caffeine, about one 8oz cup of brewed coffee. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Caffeine from coffee or tea: The caffeine comes from coffee, espresso, matcha or tea in the recipe, with no isolated caffeine added. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 10g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol, grade B or better, 10g+ protein.
