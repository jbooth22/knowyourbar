The RXBAR vs Quest decision really comes down to one question: do you care more about what's in the bar or what macros the bar delivers? They've made very different trade-offs and both are legitimate choices depending on your goals.

Quest
Quest achieves low sugar and high protein by using sucralose and erythritol as sweeteners. This lets them hit 20g protein and 3.8g net carbs in 182 calories without natural sugars. The ingredient quality score (3.9 average) reflects that sweetener reliance plus whey protein concentrate rather than isolate in some flavors. They score B or C, not A. That's not terrible, but it's a meaningful gap from RXBAR.
RXBAR
RXBAR's ingredient list is genuinely simple: egg whites, dates, nuts, and not much else. That's why 10 of 12 flavors earn an A. The cost is macros. Dates are high in natural sugar, which is why RXBARs average 14g sugar and 19.8g net carbs. That's fine if you're not tracking net carbs, but it rules them out for keto entirely. The lower protein (12.5g average) also means you'd need to eat more bars to hit a protein target.
Who should buy what

The right bar for your goal

Neither bar is better across the board. It depends entirely on what you're optimizing for.

Buy Quest if:
  • You're tracking macros and want the most protein per calorie
  • You're on keto and need to stay under 5g net carbs
  • You don't mind artificial sweeteners or sugar alcohols
  • You want more flavor options (16 vs 12)
  • You're lifting and protein is the priority above all else
Buy RXBAR if:
  • Ingredient quality matters more than hitting a specific protein number
  • You want to avoid artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols
  • You prefer real food ingredients over engineered ones
  • You're not on keto and 14g of natural sugar from dates is fine
  • You want a bar that doesn't feel like a compromise

Questions we get

Frequently asked questions

Are Quest bars or RXBARs healthier?
It depends what healthy means to you. RXBARs score significantly higher on ingredient quality. 10 of 12 flavors earn an A grade, they use whole food ingredients, and contain zero artificial sweeteners. Quest bars score B or C, use sucralose and erythritol in every flavor, but deliver 20g protein at 3.8g net carbs. If ingredient quality is your priority, RXBAR wins. If protein efficiency or keto macros matter more, Quest wins.
Which has more protein, Quest or RXBAR?
Quest bars average 19.9g protein per bar. RXBARs average 12.5g. Quest delivers significantly more protein per bar and per calorie: 10.9g per 100 calories versus 5.9g for RXBAR. If protein is the primary goal, Quest is the more efficient choice.
Do RXBARs have artificial sweeteners?
No. Zero of the 12 RXBAR flavors we scored contain sucralose, acesulfame potassium, aspartame, or saccharin. RXBARs get their sweetness from dates, which is why they have 14g of sugar per bar. That's naturally occurring sugar from the dates, not added refined sugar.
Are Quest bars keto friendly?
Yes. All 16 Quest flavors average 3.8g net carbs and hit 5g or fewer. RXBARs average 19.8g net carbs and don't qualify for keto. Quest is one of the most consistently keto-friendly bars on the market.
Which Quest or RXBAR flavor is the best?
For Quest, Oatmeal Chocolate Chip scores best on ingredient quality at 6.2 out of our scoring system. For RXBAR, Coconut Chocolate scores best at 10.3. If you want the best Quest bar for keto, Double Chocolate Chunk has the lowest net carbs at 3g. If you want the best RXBAR for protein, most flavors deliver 12g consistently.
