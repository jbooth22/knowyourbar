What makes these two bars different

Quest and RXBAR are built on different philosophies. Quest engineers its macros: high protein, very low sugar, and minimal net carbs by using artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols. That lets it hit 19.9g protein at 182 calories with 3.8g net carbs. The trade-off is ingredient quality, which sits at B or C across all 16 flavors.

RXBAR goes the other direction. Dates, egg whites, and nuts are the core of most flavors. No artificial sweeteners, no sugar alcohols. The result is an average ingredient score of 10.1 and 10 out of 12 flavors earning an A. The trade-off is macros. Dates carry natural sugar, which is why RXBARs average 14.2g sugar and 19.8g net carbs. Protein averages 12.5g, which is 37% less per bar than Quest.

Quest
Quest achieves low sugar and high protein by using sucralose and erythritol as sweeteners, plus polydextrose as a prebiotic fiber. That lets it hit 19.9g protein and 3.8g net carbs in 182 calories without natural sugars. The average ingredient score of 2.8 reflects that sweetener dependence along with the use of isolated protein fractions rather than whole food protein sources. Every flavor grades B or C, not A. That is a meaningful gap from RXBAR on ingredient quality, but it is a deliberate engineering choice, not an oversight.
RXBAR
RXBAR's classic ingredient list is simple: egg whites, dates, nuts, and not much else. That is why 10 of 12 flavors earn an A. The two B-grade flavors are the newer high-protein line, which adds pea protein and agave nectar. The cost of the whole-food approach is macros. Dates are high in natural sugar, which is why RXBARs average 14.2g sugar and 19.8g net carbs. That is fine if you are not tracking net carbs, but it rules them out for keto entirely.

The right bar for your goal

Neither brand wins outright. It depends on what you are optimizing for.

Buy Quest if:
  • You are tracking macros and want the most protein per calorie
  • You are on keto and need low net carbs (every Quest flavor lands at 5g or less)
  • Artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols are not a concern for you
  • You want more flavor variety (16 options vs 12)
  • You are lifting and protein is the priority above all else
Buy RXBAR if:
  • Ingredient quality matters more than hitting a specific protein target
  • You want to avoid artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohols entirely
  • You prefer whole food ingredients over engineered formulas
  • You are not on keto and about 14g of natural sugar from dates works for you
  • You want a bar that reads like a recipe, not a supplement label

Bottom line

The Quest vs RXBAR decision comes down to one question: do you care more about what is in the bar or what macros the bar delivers? Quest engineers macros. RXBAR sources ingredients. Both are legitimate choices depending on your goals, and neither is obviously wrong for the person it is designed for.

If you are counting net carbs, need 20g protein per bar, or are on keto, Quest wins on almost every macro metric. If you are avoiding artificial sweeteners, prefer whole food ingredients, and do not need the bar to double as a protein supplement, RXBAR is the better choice. The ingredient quality difference (10.1 vs 2.8) is substantial. That gap reflects real differences in what you are eating.
