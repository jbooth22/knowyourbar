Are KIND Bars Healthy? We Ranked All 31 Flavors

KIND doesn't market this as sub-brands in our database, but the ingredients show two distinct formulas hiding under one label. The 7 flavors built on allulose (the four bars that used to carry the separate "Protein Max" name, plus the three Nuts and Sea Salt minis) all grade B, no exceptions. The other 24 flavors, sweetened with honey, cane sugar, or glucose syrup, span B down to the lineup's only D grade. Protein swings just as widely, from 3g in a snack-sized fruit bar to 20g in the allulose-based bars, which drags the lineup average down to 8.1g, bottom 23% of every bar we track. Sugar alcohol and artificial sweeteners are a non-issue: zero across all 31 flavors. Here is the full breakdown, flavor by flavor.

What the data shows across all 31 KIND flavors

The clearest split in the KIND lineup isn't a marketing line, it's the sweetener. The 7 flavors built on allulose, which used to carry the separate "KIND Protein Max" name on 4 of them plus 3 Nuts and Sea Salt minis, average a score of 5.9 and grade B across the board, no exceptions. The other 24 flavors, sweetened with honey, cane sugar, or glucose syrup instead, average a score of 3.4 and span B down to the lineup's only D grade (Dark Chocolate Mocha Almond). Protein tracks the same split: the allulose flavors average 13.6g, the honey and cane sugar flavors average 6.5g.

That second group isn't one thing either. Ten of its flavors are oat-and-soy Breakfast bars that mostly skip palm kernel oil (2/10 use it) and hold their scores up close to the allulose group. The other 14 are classic nut-and-fruit bars built on honey, cane sugar, and glucose syrup stacked together, and palm kernel oil shows up in 8 of those 14. That combination, more sweetener sources plus more palm oil, is what pulls the group average down and produces the lineup's only D grade.

What KIND does consistently, across every one of the 31 flavors, is skip artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohol entirely. Zero flavors use either. Certifications are common too: 30 of 31 flavors are Gluten Free, 9 are Non-GMO, and 13 are Kosher.

Processed Oils is the most common concern, flagging 25 of 31 flavors. Palm kernel oil specifically shows up in 17 of 31, all 7 allulose-based flavors and 10 of the 24 honey and cane sugar flavors; the chip also catches canola and other refined oils in 8 more flavors, mostly from the oat-and-soy Breakfast line. Sweetener Heavy hits 19 of 31 for stacking multiple sweetening agents. On the positive side, Whole Food Forward credits 15 flavors for leading with real nuts, seeds, or fruit, and Quality Protein Source recognizes 14 flavors for a genuine protein source rather than a filler blend. Fortified, a neutral chip noting added vitamins, shows up in 12 flavors.

Bottom line on KIND

KIND's real strength is what it leaves out. Every one of the 31 flavors we've scored skips artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohol, a combination no other brand we've reviewed has matched across its entire lineup. But "KIND bars" isn't one product from an ingredient standpoint. The 7 allulose-based flavors, which include the four bars that used to carry the separate "Protein Max" name, all grade B and carry meaningfully more protein (13.6g average) than the rest of the lineup. The other 24 flavors, sweetened with honey and cane sugar instead, average less than half that protein and span all the way down to the lineup's only D grade.

If you're buying KIND for protein, the allulose-based flavors are where that case actually holds up; the honey-sweetened classic bars are closer to a fortified snack bar than a protein bar. Start with Dark Chocolate Nuts and Sea Salt if ingredient quality is the deciding factor, or Raspberry Cocoa Crisp if you want the full 20g protein format at the best available grade.

Which KIND flavor should you actually buy

With a 31-flavor lineup spanning two different sweetener formulas, there isn't one answer to "which KIND bar." Here's how we'd sort it depending on your goals, based on what's actually in the data above rather than the best- or worst-flavor snapshot alone.

Best overall ingredient quality
Dark Chocolate Nuts and Sea Salt

Grades B at 7.7, the highest score in the lineup, built on almonds, peanuts, and brown rice with allulose instead of honey or cane sugar.

Full 20g protein, best grade
Raspberry Cocoa Crisp

All four of the former Protein Max flavors carry 20g protein at 240-250 calories. Raspberry Cocoa Crisp grades highest of that group at B (5.9), the one to start with if you want the full protein-bar format.

Best of the honey-sweetened flavors
Peanut Butter

Sweetened with cane sugar and tapioca syrup like most of the classic lineup, but it skips palm kernel oil in favor of canola oil, which keeps it at B (6.5), the best score of the 24 non-allulose flavors.

Skip regardless of format
Dark Chocolate Mocha Almond
Cranberry Almond

Dark Chocolate Mocha Almond is the lineup's only D grade. Cranberry Almond isn't far behind at C (0.2), the lowest-scoring flavor with no positive chip at all: just almonds, dried cranberries, and three separate sweeteners.
