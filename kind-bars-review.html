Are KIND Bars Healthy? We Ranked All 31 Flavors

KIND doesn't market this as sub-brands, but the ingredients show three distinct formulas under one label: 7 allulose-sweetened bars (the four 20g-protein bars that used to carry the "Protein Max" name, plus three Nuts and Sea Salt minis), 8 oat-and-soy breakfast bars, and 16 classic nut-and-fruit bars sweetened with honey, cane sugar, and glucose syrup. Every flavor grades B or C, with no A and nothing lower. Protein swings from 3g in a snack-sized bar to 20g in the allulose bars, which drags the lineup average down to 8.1g, bottom 23% of every bar we track. Sugar alcohol and artificial sweeteners are a non-issue: zero across all 31 flavors. Here is the full breakdown, flavor by flavor.

KIND's clearest strength is what it skips: zero sugar alcohol and zero artificial sweeteners across all 31 flavors. The tradeoff shows up in protein. At 8.1g average, KIND lands in the bottom 23% of every bar we track, but that single number hides a real split: the 7 allulose flavors average 13.6g protein, the 8 breakfast bars 8.0g, and the 16 classic nut bars just 5.7g.

What the data shows across all 31 KIND flavors

KIND's lineup splits into three formulas, and the split is mostly about protein and oil, not grade. Grade barely follows the formula: the 7 allulose flavors average a score of 4.7 (B to C), the 8 breakfast bars 4.8 (B to C), and the 16 classic nut bars 4.0 (B to C). Protein is where the formulas really differ: 13.6g, 8.0g, and 5.7g on average.

Each formula carries a different concern. All 7 allulose flavors use palm kernel oil and stack sweetening agents (Sweetener Heavy on 7 of 7). The breakfast bars skip palm kernel oil but use canola, and all 8 stack sweeteners. The classic nut bars are the most whole-food of the three, with Whole Food Forward on 14 of 16, but palm kernel oil shows up in 10 of them.

What KIND does consistently, across every one of the 31 flavors, is skip artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohol entirely. Certifications are common too: 30 of 31 flavors are Gluten Free, 9 are Non-GMO, and 13 are Kosher.

Processed Oils is the most common concern, flagging 25 of 31 flavors. Palm kernel oil specifically shows up in 17 of 31: all 7 allulose flavors and 10 of the 16 classic nut bars. The chip also catches canola in the 8 breakfast bars that skip palm. Sweetener Heavy hits 19 of 31 for stacking multiple sweetening agents. On the positive side, Whole Food Forward credits 16 flavors for leading with real nuts, seeds, or fruit, and Quality Protein Source recognizes 14 flavors for a genuine protein source. Fortified, a neutral chip noting added vitamins, shows up in 12 flavors.

Bottom line on KIND

KIND's real strength is what it leaves out. Every one of the 31 flavors we've scored skips artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohol, something only RXBAR and Clif also manage among the brands we've reviewed. But "KIND bars" isn't one product. The four 20g-protein allulose bars carry real protein; the 16 classic nut bars average 5.7g and are closer to a snack bar than a protein bar.

If you're buying KIND for protein, the 20g allulose bars are where that case actually holds up. Start with Dark Chocolate Cherry Cashew if ingredient quality is the deciding factor, or Raspberry Cocoa Crisp if you want the full 20g protein format at the best available grade.

Frequently asked questions about KIND bars

KIND grades B to C across all 31 flavors we've scored, with no A's and nothing below C. The lineup splits into three formulas: 7 allulose-sweetened bars (the four 20g-protein bars that used to carry the "Protein Max" name, plus three Nuts and Sea Salt minis), 8 oat-and-soy breakfast bars, and 16 classic nut-and-fruit bars sweetened with honey, cane sugar, and glucose syrup. Every flavor skips artificial sweeteners and sugar alcohol entirely, but protein is low outside the 20g bars.
Dark Chocolate Cherry Cashew grades highest at B (score 6.9), built on cashews, peanuts, and chicory root fiber.
Protein ranges from 3g to 20g per bar, averaging 8.1g, which lands in the bottom 23% of every bar we track. That average is misleading on its own: the 7 allulose flavors average 13.6g, the 8 breakfast bars 8.0g, and the 16 classic nut bars just 5.7g.

KIND's protein per 100 calories, 3.9g, is the lowest of the brands we compare it against, behind Quest (11.0g), Barebells (8.9g), and RXBAR (5.9g). Its fiber average, 5.3g, is the second-highest of that group, and it shares zero sugar alcohol and zero artificial sweeteners with RXBAR.
KIND relies on nuts, seeds, and soy protein isolate rather than whey or casein. The 20g allulose bars lead with peanuts and soy protein isolate, and the breakfast bars add soy protein isolate alongside oats.
The four flavors that used to carry the separate "KIND Protein Max" name (Raspberry Cocoa Crisp, Dark Chocolate Crisp, Crispy Chocolate Peanut Butter, and Sweet and Salty Caramel Peanut Crisp) are now folded into the main KIND lineup in our database. They're still a distinct formula on the shelf: 62g bars built around allulose with 20g of protein, versus the 35-50g classic and breakfast bars that run 3-12g of protein.
17 of 31 flavors list palm kernel oil directly: all 7 allulose flavors and 10 of the 16 classic nut bars. The broader Processed Oils concern chip covers 25 of 31, since it also flags the canola oil in the breakfast bars that skip palm.
30 of the 31 KIND flavors we've scored are labeled Gluten Free. Cranberry Almond is the only flavor without that label in our data, though that reflects a missing certification flag rather than a confirmed gluten-containing ingredient.
