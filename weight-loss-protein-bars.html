The 10 Best Protein Bars for Weight Loss

Of the 1,000+ bars we track, 154 pass all four, about 12%. Sugar knocks out the most, and big protein usually comes with big calories: 70% of bars with 20g+ protein are over 200 calories. We are not doctors or dietitians. These are the qualities we know people look for, so that's what we factored in.

What makes a protein bar good for weight loss

Losing weight comes down to eating fewer calories than you burn, so a protein bar only helps if it replaces something heavier or keeps you from reaching for something else. That makes the useful question simple: how filling is it for its calories? We screened every bar for four things: 15g or more protein, 200 calories or less, 5g of sugar or less, and at least 3g of fiber.

154 of 1,000+ bars pass all four. Here is how often each check catches a bar.

We are not doctors or dietitians. These are the qualities we know people look for, so that's what we factored in.
Over 200 calories
644 bars49%
Plenty of bars sold as protein bars carry 250 to 400 calories, which is a small meal, not a snack.
Under 15g protein
699 bars53%
Protein is the most filling of the three macros. Under 15g, a bar is mostly carbs and fat.
Over 5g sugar
736 bars56%
Sugar adds calories without keeping you full. We set the line at 5g per bar.
Under 3g fiber
332 bars25%
Fiber slows digestion and helps a snack hold you over until the next meal.

Sugar alcohols are allowed here

We don't exclude sugar alcohols on this page. They add few calories, and that is the point of this screen. But 102 of the 154 bars that pass (66%) reach their low sugar number with one, and erythritol, maltitol and the like upset some people's stomachs. If they bother you, check the label or start from our no sugar alcohols guide. Our Lowest sugar pick only considers bars without one.

Weight loss doesn't screen on ingredient grade

The four checks are about calories and fullness, not what the bar is made of, so 119 of the 154 bars that pass grade C or below. Our A to F grade measures ingredient quality and is a separate question. Every bar in the Best 10 below grades A or B, and the list further down puts A and B bars first.

A bar is still food

A 190-calorie bar that replaces a 400-calorie pastry helps. The same bar eaten on top of everything else doesn't. Bars here average 177 calories, 17.7g of protein and 7.8g of fiber. Everyone's needs are different, and nothing on this page replaces advice from your doctor or a registered dietitian.

What we found screening 1,000+ bars

Sugar is the most common miss.
736 bars (56%) have more than 5g of sugar, ahead of protein (53% under 15g), calories (49% over 200) and fiber (25% under 3g). 824 of the 1,154 bars that fail miss more than one check.
Big protein usually means big calories.
210 of the 301 bars with 20g+ protein (70%) are over 200 calories. A high protein number on the wrapper says nothing about the calories it came with, so check both.
Clean bars rarely fit the numbers.
Only 2% of A-grade bars pass all four checks, and most of them miss on protein: 141 of the 197 A-grade bars have under 15g. Ingredient quality and weight loss numbers are separate questions.

How we picked these

What qualifies: 15g or more protein, 200 calories or less, 5g or less sugar, and 3g or more fiber. No grade check and no sugar alcohol check on the list itself. 154 of the 1,000+ bars we track qualify.

Rules for every pick:

  • The bar has to qualify for this guide, carry an A or B ingredient grade, and have at least 10g of protein.
  • We rank ingredient quality by grade only. Two bars with the same grade count as equal, because our scoring isn't precise enough to split them. When two bars tie on a pick's own number and grade, the one you can buy through a link on this page goes first (a brand's own partner link, then Amazon). After that, ties go to more protein, then less sugar, then more fiber, then fewer calories.
  • No bar appears twice in the Best 10, and no brand gets more than 2 of the 10 spots.
  • If a pick's rule finds no eligible bar, that spot goes to the next rule on this guide's backup list, so there are always 10.
  • Nobody pays for a spot. Every bar goes through the same rules. Buy links may earn us a commission. A link only ever decides between bars that are already tied, and never lifts a bar over one with a better grade or a better number.

How each pick was chosen:

  • Best overall: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the most protein per 100 calories, with 15g+ protein.
  • Cleanest ingredients: An A-grade bar (B if no A qualifies) with the shortest ingredient list. Every bar in the same grade is treated as equal on ingredient quality, so the tiebreak is a label you can count.
  • Highest protein: The most protein per bar, grade B or better, capped at 300 calories so a large candy-style bar cannot win on size alone.
  • Most protein per calorie: The most protein per 100 calories, grade B or better, 12g+ protein.
  • Lowest calorie: The best ingredient grade on the list, then the fewest calories, 10g+ protein.
  • Best from a big brand: The Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), limited to brands with national grocery, big-box or Costco distribution. 10g+ protein.
  • Best whey protein: Whey protein on the label. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
  • Lowest sugar: The least sugar among bars with no sugar alcohol (none in the ingredients and 0g on the label), grade B or better, 10g+ protein. Skipping sugar alcohols keeps a bar from winning on a low sugar line it reached with one.
  • Best dairy-free: Labeled dairy free by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
  • Best non-GMO: Labeled non-GMO by the brand. Ranked with the Best overall rule (best grade, then most protein per 100 calories), 15g+ protein.
